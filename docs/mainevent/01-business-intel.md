# Main Event Entertainment: Business Intelligence

**Purpose.** Ground truth for an interactive work sample supporting Nathan J. Song's application for **Sales Manager (Event Sales Manager), Main Event Brea, CA, $75,662 to $89,014**. The role drives event revenue through consultative selling, outbound prospecting, cold calling, and retention of an existing client base, reporting to the General Manager.

**Compiled** 11 August 2026. Every factual claim carries a source URL. Anything inferred is labelled **[INFERENCE]**. Anything unverified is labelled and repeated in Section 9. **No price, headcount, or benchmark in this document was invented.** Where a number is not published, the document says so.

**The single most important finding is in Section 2.2.** The Main Event Brea location page is a pre-opening page whose entire body copy is boilerplate from a Tempe, Arizona location. It describes Brea as being "off I-10 and Warner Road," "20 minutes from downtown Phoenix," and offers to help you "take a break from the desert heat." That is a live, verifiable defect on the employer's own website for the exact site being hired for. It is the strongest available hook for a work sample.

### 2.2 The Tempe boilerplate defect (verify before building)

The Brea location page's **meta description**, **Open Graph description**, and **two full body paragraphs** are copy from an Arizona location that was never localised. Verbatim from [mainevent.com/locations/california/brea](https://www.mainevent.com/locations/california/brea/):

> "Main Event in Brea CA is located off I-10 and Warner Road, just west of South Priest Drive. 20 minutes from downtown Phoenix. When you're looking for fun things do in Tempe, bowling or the best sports bar, we've got you covered." (meta description)

> "Main Event in Brea, California, is located off I-10 and Warner Road, just west of South Priest Drive. **We're part of the Brea metro area; near the cities of Phoenix and Chandler.** We're located 20 minutes from downtown Phoenix and 10 minutes from US 60... You can **take a break from the desert heat** at our bar..."

> "When you're looking for the perfect birthday party destination for your little one **in the Phoenix metropolitan area**, look no further than Brea's Main Event."

I-10, Warner Road and South Priest Drive are in **Tempe, Arizona**. Brea, California is served by **SR 57**, and is 25 miles southeast of Los Angeles ([Wikipedia: Brea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brea,_California)).

**Consequences for the work sample:**
1. Every SEO signal on the page points at Phoenix. A Brea family or a Fullerton HR manager searching "birthday party Brea" or "corporate event Brea" hits a page selling Tempe.
2. The **"more than 26 lanes"** and **"over 100 games"** figures in that same paragraph block are **part of the Tempe boilerplate and must be treated as unverified for Brea.** The independently sourced attraction list is the WhatNow one: bowling, laser tag, 120+ arcade games, restaurant and bar.
3. The Brea page's enquiry form is a **pre-opening interest form**, not the standard group events form. It collects First Name*, Last Name*, Email*, Phone (not required), **Company / Organization / Group**, Additional Information*, and a consent checkbox. It does **not** ask for event date, guest count, or event type.

**[INFERENCE]** For a pre-opening centre, the pre-opening enquiry list is the entire day-one pipeline. A form that does not capture date, headcount, or event type produces leads that cannot be qualified, sequenced, or forecast. That is the gap a work sample should close.

**Party seasonality, source type: vendor-published (ROLLER, 2025).** Verbatim: *"In AMER and EMEA, September, October, November, and January are the busiest months for party sales"* and *"In contrast, December tends to be a slower month for party bookings across all regions."* ([roller.software](https://www.roller.software/blog/party-booking-insights-from-the-2025-attractions-industry-benchmark-report))

**The operationally useful tension: December is peak corporate party season and trough birthday party season.** Main Event's own January-shoulder discount (Section 4.6) is the commercial response to exactly this.

### 7.7 Speed to lead (the best-evidenced lever available)

**Primary research, source type: academic study published in business press.**

**Harvard Business Review, "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads," Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington, March 2011** ([hbr.org](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads), abstract; full text via [this PDF copy](https://thedenmangroupselling.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads-harvard-business-review.pdf)):

> "We audited 2,241 U.S. companies, measuring how long each took to respond to a web-generated test lead. Although 37% responded to their lead within an hour, and 16% responded within one to 24 hours, 24% took more than 24 hours, and 23% of the companies never responded at all. The average response time, among companies that responded within 30 days, was 42 hours."

> "Firms that tried to contact potential customers within an hour of receiving a query were nearly **seven times** as likely to qualify the lead... as those that tried to contact the customer even an hour later, and more than **60 times** as likely as companies that waited 24 hours or longer."

**Correction worth knowing: the famous "5 minutes / 21x / 100x" figures are NOT in the HBR paper.** They come from Oldroyd's separate **2007 Lead Response Management Study**, quoted verbatim in the [InsideSales/XANT 2014 Lead Response Report, p.5](https://resources.insidesales.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/2014-Lead-Response-Report.pdf) (vendor-published): *"the odds of making a successful contact with a lead are 100 times greater when a contact attempt occurs within 5 minutes, compared to 30 minutes after the lead was submitted. Similarly, the odds of the lead entering the sales process, or becoming qualified, are 21 times greater when contacted within 5 minutes versus 30 minutes."*

**XANT/InsideSales 2014 Lead Response Report** (vendor-published, 2013 data, 14,061 companies audited, 9,538 receiving a test lead), same URL:
- **47%** of responding companies did not respond to the submitted lead at all (4,472 of 9,538).
- **Median first call response time 3 hours 8 minutes; average 61 hours 1 minute.**
- **70%** of companies respond first by email, **30%** by phone.
- **Median number of contact attempts: 1. Average: 2.2.**

**Venue-specific, source type: vendor-published.**
- **77% of consumers consider a venue's responsiveness highly important** when booking (42% "very important," 35% "extremely important"). Methodology: Tripleseat surveyed 500 consumers. ([Tripleseat](https://tripleseat.com/articles/venue-responsiveness-reigns-supreme-why-lightning-fast-communication-is-your-venues-secret-weapon/), July 2025)
- **74% of consumers use technology for some or all of their event booking process.** Same URL.
- **80% of planners say the ideal RFP response time is four days or less** ([Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/en/blog/hospitality/hotel-group-sales), 2025).
- **49% of planners now source special event venues, up from 17% in 2023.** Same Cvent URL. Useful for arguing FECs are entering the corporate consideration set.
- Tripleseat's recommended SLA, guidance not measured data: *"the most successful venues aim to respond within one hour during business hours."*

**A venue-specific mystery-shop study of response times: NOT FOUND.** The 18%, 28.3%, 55% and 63.5% non-response figures circulating online come from car dealership and B2B SaaS studies, not venues.

## 10. Five things a builder should take from this

**[INFERENCE throughout this section. These are conclusions, not sourced facts.]**

1. **The Brea page is broken and the fix is the pitch.** A pre-opening location page selling Tempe, Arizona, with a form that captures no date, headcount or event type, is the single most defensible thing to rebuild in a work sample. It is verifiable, it is the exact site being hired for, and fixing it is literally the job.

2. **Nobody in this market publishes group pricing, so speed is the only available edge.** Of ~45 venues checked, ten publish a per-person group rate. The rest route to a form. The best-evidenced lever in Section 7.7 is response time, and it is backed by an HBR study, not a content farm. A work sample that models speed-to-lead is built on the strongest evidence available.

3. **Price against Alta Vista Country Club, not against the escape rooms.** A north-OC company pays roughly $65 to $110 all-in per head for a sit-down corporate dinner. Main Event's job is not to be cheaper than a $37 escape room; it is to be a better use of the same banquet budget.
