# Event Sales Funnel Research: Family Entertainment Centres

Working reference for the Main Event Entertainment (Brea, CA) Event Sales Manager work sample.
Compiled 2026-08-11. Every claim carries a source URL.

**Labels used throughout**
- **[Vendor]** = figure published by a software vendor, agency, or operator with a commercial interest in the number. Directionally useful, not audited.
- **[Operator]** = published by the venue or brand itself (pricing pages, fundraiser terms, press releases).
- **[Filing]** = SEC filing or earnings call. Highest confidence.
- **[Study]** = named academic or research study.
- **[Inference]** = my reasoning, not sourced. Flagged so it never gets presented as fact.

## 0. Why this matters for the build

Dave & Buster's, which owns Main Event, states in its 10-K that the special events business is important **because a significant percentage of guests attending a special event are visiting for the first time**, making it a sampling and promotional channel, not just a revenue line. Special events were **9.8% of total revenues in fiscal 2018**. [Filing] ([D&B FY2018 10-K, SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001525769/000119312518106389/d407284d10k.htm))

In Q1 FY2026, D&B reported special events grew roughly **3%** year over year while F&B same-store sales rose about **5%**. [Filing] ([earnings call transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-dave--busters-q1-2026-miss-sends-shares-lower-93CH-4743490)) The company has publicly named "optimized online booking and entertainment offerings tailored towards group events" as a growth lever. [Filing] ([D&B FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1525769/000152576926000020/a2025ars10-k.pdf))

**This is the single strongest argument for the whole work sample:** the group funnel and the family funnel are not two businesses. A booked group event is a mass first-visit acquisition event for the consumer business. Every corporate party, team night, and school fundraiser dumps 30 to 200 new people into the building who have never been. The job of the Event Sales Manager is not only to close the event. It is to convert the room.

## 1. The two funnels, and how they differ

### 1.1 Side-by-side

| | **Consumer family funnel** | **B2B / group funnel** |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A date on the calendar (child's birthday), bad weather, school break, a free Saturday, a coupon or season pass already owned | A budget line, a fiscal quarter, a season ending, a holiday, an HR mandate, a fundraising goal |
| **Who researches** | The parent, usually the mother, often after a kid names the venue | Executive assistant, office manager, HR, coach, team parent, PTA/PTO chair, teacher, youth pastor |
| **Decision maker** | Same person as researcher; sometimes the child has veto | Often NOT the researcher. EA researches, VP approves. Team parent researches, coach or league approves. |
| **Research behaviour** | Google "kids birthday party near me", compares 2 to 4 venues on price and vibe, reads reviews, wants pricing visible immediately | Compares availability first, then capacity, then price per head; needs a proposal document they can forward to an approver |
| **Lead time** | Birthday parties commonly booked 2 to 3 months out in peak season [Vendor] ([Party Center Software](https://www.partycentersoftware.com/blog/party-scheduling-tips)); walk-in family visits are same-day | Corporate holiday parties: mid-size firms start 4 to 6 months out, Fortune 500s 9 to 12 months [Vendor] ([SPIN](https://wearespin.com/how-far-in-advance-should-you-book-office-party-venues/)). Routine corporate dinners: 2 to 4 weeks [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) |
| **Price sensitivity** | High. Personal money. Per-head price is compared line by line. | Lower on line items, because it is a business expense. Sensitive to total vs approved budget. [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) |
| **Booking channel** | Self-serve online. Main Event sells birthdays, activities, and the Season Pass directly through mainevent.com/book [Operator] ([Main Event](https://www.mainevent.com/book/)) | Lead form to a human. Main Event's group form asks name, location, date, "I'd like to book…", attendee count, plus opt-ins for multi-location events and a **free tour of the center** [Operator] ([Main Event group events](https://www.mainevent.com/events/group-events/)) |
| **What actually closes them** | Instant price, date availability, a package that feels like it was designed for their kid's age, and social proof | Speed of first reply, a clean proposal an approver will sign off on, the site tour, and a named human who owns the account |
| **Repeat behaviour** | A birthday repeats once a year, if that. Direct repeat rate for social events "approaches zero" [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) | A corporate account can produce 4 to 12 bookings a year; lifetime value 4x to 12x a single social booking [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) |
| **Day of week** | Friday, Saturday, Sunday | Tuesday to Thursday, Thursday peak [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) |
| **Average value** | ~$2,000 median for a birthday-type booking [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) | ~$4,400+ for corporate at comparable headcount [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/corporate-vs-social)) |

[Operator] ([Main Event Level Up](https://www.mainevent.com/events/corporate-events/level-up/))
- Capacity range marketed as **10 to 2,000 guests**, with full facility buyout available. [Operator] ([Main Event corporate](https://www.mainevent.com/events/corporate-events/full-facility-buyout-corporate/))
- Third-party venue directories list Main Event party spend for 50 guests ranging roughly **$600 to $3,950** depending on market. [Vendor] ([Eventective Atlanta](https://www.eventective.com/atlanta-ga/main-event-entertainment-atlanta-664363.html), [Eventective Independence MO](https://www.eventective.com/independence-mo/main-event-768062.html))

### 1.2 The critical asymmetry

The consumer funnel is a **conversion problem**: traffic already exists, the job is to remove friction. The group funnel is a **pipeline problem**: the demand does not exist until someone creates it, and the same 200 target accounts in a trade area get called by every venue.

**[Inference]** This is why the work sample should show two visually distinct funnels that meet at a shared bottom. The family funnel narrows fast and self-serves. The group funnel is long, human, and stage-gated. The shared bottom is the guest database: every group attendee becomes a consumer lead.

## 2. The outbound motion this job describes

### 2.1 What the posting actually names

Published summaries of the Main Event Event Sales Manager role describe: driving event revenue through consultative selling, outbound prospecting, and retaining an existing client base; performing **outbound lead-generating activities outside the building including tabling, networking events, and go-sees** with prospective and current customers; developing a local and outbound sales strategy to identify high-potential target segments and industries; building and managing high-performing sales teams; managing the full sales cycle through prospecting, calls, and setting appointments; and **tracking progress on daily, weekly, and monthly outbound prospecting and sales goals in CRM systems**. [Vendor/aggregator] ([Main Event Brea listing summary via LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/event-sales-manager-at-main-event-4442693738); [D&B on-premise Event Sales Manager listing, Lensa](https://lensa.com/job-v1/dave-buster/college-station-tx/event-sales-manager/27b8082399d39993a69f63df7776808d); [Main Event careers](https://www.mainevent.com/careers/opportunities/))

Definitions worth getting right in the build:
- **Tabling** = staffing a branded table at someone else's gathering (a school open house, a chamber mixer, a youth sports registration day, a corporate benefits fair) to collect leads.
- **Go-see** = an in-person drop-in on a prospect or existing client at their office, with something in hand (menu, promo card, gift card). The hospitality equivalent of a field visit.
- **Site tour / free tour of the center** = the venue's version of a demo. Main Event's own group form offers it as a checkbox. [Operator] ([Main Event](https://www.mainevent.com/events/group-events/))

### 2.3 Standard event sales pipeline stages

The clearest published stage model, with pass-through rates, is Sway's. **[Vendor]** ([Sway conversion benchmarks](https://www.trysway.co/blog/conversion-benchmarks))

| Stage | Meaning | Typical pass-through |
|---|---|---|
| Inquiry received | every lead from every source | 100% |
| First reply sent | a real human response goes out | should be 100%, often 85 to 90% |
| Qualified and engaged | host replies, confirms date, size, format, budget | 40 to 50% of all inquiries |
| Proposal sent | pricing, options, availability delivered | 60 to 70% of qualified |
| Confirmed booking | proposal accepted, deposit paid, on the calendar | 35 to 45% of proposals |

Sway's own portfolio result: **about 1 in 3 resolved/qualified inquiries becomes a confirmed event (34% close rate)**. They cite HubSpot benchmarks treating 20 to 30% on qualified leads as strong, and state most venues working their own inquiries land under 15%. [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/conversion-benchmarks))

Sway's self-assessment tiers, useful as a scoring rubric in an interactive build [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/conversion-benchmarks)):
- **Strong**: high-teens to low-20s close on all inquiries, first reply inside an hour, proposal close above 45%, 25+ inquiries/month
- **Solid**: low-to-mid teens close, same-business-day reply, proposal close 35 to 45%, 15 to 25 inquiries/month
- **Struggling**: under 8% close, next-business-day reply or worse, proposal close under 30%, under 15 inquiries/month

An outbound pipeline needs stages **before** inquiry that the inbound model above does not have. **[Inference]**, but consistent with the posting's own language: Target list built → Contacted (cold call, email, LinkedIn) → Conversation held → Appointment set / tour booked → Qualified opportunity → Proposal → Deposit → Held → Rebooked next year.

### 2.4 Speed to lead

The single most transferable published finding:
- **MIT / InsideSales Lead Response Management Study** (Oldroyd et al., 15,000+ leads, 100,000+ call attempts, 2004 to 2007): contacting a web lead within **5 minutes** makes it about **21x** more likely to qualify than waiting 30 minutes. Summarised later in HBR (2011) as "The Short Life of Online Sales Leads." [Study] ([MIT study PDF](https://25649.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na2.net/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf); [attribution note](https://ainora.lt/blog/lead-response-time-5-minutes-study-2026))
- Venue-specific: venues replying within an hour close at "roughly double" the rate of next-day repliers. [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/conversion-benchmarks))
- Wedding venue median first-reply time measured at **11 hours**. [Vendor] ([EveryBooking](https://everybooking.com/blog/wedding-venue-inquiry-response-time-benchmark))
- 30 minutes is cited as a working industry benchmark for first response. [Vendor] ([VenueQuoter](https://venuequoter.com/blog/key-venue-inquiry-response-time-questions-answered))

### 2.5 What a week plausibly looks like

**[Inference]**, assembled from the posting language plus the seasonality data in section 3. Present it as a proposed operating rhythm, not a fact.

| Day | Shape |
|---|---|
| Mon | Pipeline hygiene in CRM, weekend inquiry cleanup, week's target list built, proposals out from Friday inquiries |
| Tue | Heaviest cold call block (corporate contacts are at desks, Tue to Thu is the corporate booking window) |

### 3.2 The full-year demand curve

Sway publishes a five-season model from its venue portfolio. [Vendor] ([Sway seasonal demand](https://www.trysway.co/blog/seasonal-demand))

| Window | Shape | Sales implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct to early Dec** | The surge. Highest volume of the year, 10-week compression. **October is consistently the highest-inquiry month.** | If your marketing ramps in November you are late. Hosts are comparing venues in September. |
| **Jan to Feb** | The cliff. Inquiry volume drops **40 to 60%** from October. | Not dead. January is when corporate planners finalise the year's calendar. Q1 inquiries are often for larger Q2/Q3 events. |
| **Mar to May** | The build. Corporate Q1 wraps, spring celebrations, graduation late April into May. | May graduation weekends can carry a 10 to 15% premium in university markets. |
| **Jun to Aug** | The split. Varies hard by venue type. | For an indoor, air-conditioned FEC in Southern California this is arguably peak family season, the opposite of the urban corporate venue pattern. **[Inference]** |
| **Sep** | The second surge. Second-highest booking month, shorter lead time (2 to 3 weeks vs 4 to 6 for holiday). | Commonly mispriced as "summer." |

### 3.3 Lead time distribution

From Sway's confirmed-booking set. [Vendor] ([Sway booking timing](https://www.trysway.co/blog/booking-timing))

| Booked before event | Share of confirmed events |
|---|---|
| 0 to 7 days | 22% |
| 1 to 4 weeks | 26% |
| 1 to 2 months | 17% |
| 2 to 3 months | 14% |
| 3 to 6 months | 12% |
| 6 months or more | 10% |

Median gap from confirmation to event: **36 days**. **48%** of confirmed events lock within 30 days. But the **inquiry** typically arrives around **8 weeks** before the event. Median complete confirmed booking value: **$2,625**. [Vendor] ([Sway](https://www.trysway.co/blog/private-event-benchmarks))

The operational lesson Sway draws is the one worth putting in the build: **a thin month four weeks out is mostly already written; a thin quarter is a decision you have not made yet.** Near-term gaps get filled by reworking dead inquiries and repeat clients, not by new marketing.

### 4.2 The structural pattern

Three distinct models, and they solve different problems. **[Inference]** on the categorisation, the facts underneath are sourced above.

1. **Subscription** (Chuck E. Cheese Fun Pass, Sky Zone, Urban Air). Converts an occasion purchase into recurring revenue and a 12-month commitment. Highest retention lock-in, hardest sell.
2. **Season pass** (Main Event, Bowlero). A one-time payment that pre-buys a season and reframes every subsequent visit as free. Solves the *seasonal trough*, not annual retention.
3. **Points and status** (D&B Rewards). Free to join, so it is really a **data capture and identification** mechanism first and a retention mechanism second. It tells you who a guest is and what they play.

Main Event sits in category 2 and, through D&B Rewards, has access to category 3. It has **no** category 1 product. **[Inference]**

### 4.3 Published evidence that these work

Thin, and I will not pretend otherwise. What exists:
- Operators state directly that memberships, loyalty rewards and return-visit incentives are used to smooth seasonal troughs. [Vendor] ([BMI Leisure](https://bmileisure.com/family-entertainment-center-marketing/))
- Sky Zone publishes member testimonial framing around habitual repeat visits ("gets us off the couch and jumping together multiple times every month"). [Operator] ([Sky Zone](https://www.skyzone.com/group-events/))
- Chuck E. Cheese's 12-month minimum commitment plus $10 initiation fee is itself evidence the economics work at that price point, because no operator locks in a 12-month term on a product that loses money. **[Inference]**

**I could not find** audited retention lift, redemption rates, or member-vs-non-member frequency data for any of these programs. See section 9.

## 5. The family group occasion (the growth idea)

The thesis: the highest-value family visit is not one family. It is a family that brings other families.

### 5.1 What the demand data says

Multigenerational and extended-family group activity is measurably rising in the adjacent travel category:
- American Express Travel's 2025 Global Travel Trends Report: **58% of millennial and Gen Z parents** planned to bring extended family on vacation in 2025; **57% of parents** planned to travel with grandparents and children, up from 55% in 2023. [Vendor] ([Hospitality On summary](https://hospitality-on.com/en/hospitality/changing-face-family-travel-connection-multigenerational-experiences), [VAX Vacation Access](https://www.vaxvacationaccess.com/the-compass/posts/family-travel-trends-to-watch-for-in-2025/))
- **89%** cited quality time as the main reason for inviting the whole family; **81%** prefer destinations with activities that appeal to all ages. [Vendor] ([Hospitality On](https://hospitality-on.com/en/hospitality/changing-face-family-travel-connection-multigenerational-experiences))
- 2025 NYU SPS Family Travel Survey documents the rise of "kidfluence" in family booking decisions. [Study-adjacent] ([NYU SPS](https://www.sps.nyu.edu/about/news-and-ideas/articles/press-releases/2025/2025-family-travel-survey-highlights-rise-of-kidfluence.html))

**[Inference]**: an indoor FEC with bowling, laser tag, ropes and arcade is unusually well suited to the multigenerational occasion because it has activities with genuinely different physical demands under one roof. Grandparents bowl, teens do laser tag, small kids do arcade. That is a real product advantage and nobody in the category is selling it.

| Picnic / large seasonal group | Company or community organiser | Main Event ran a Summer Picnics promotion for guest lists of **200 to 2,000** with a free upgrade for booking by Aug 31 [Operator] ([Main Event](https://www.mainevent.com/events/group-events/)) |

### 5.3 The gap

Every one of these is already sold **as a B2B lead form**. None is sold as a **consumer product a parent can buy in 90 seconds**. Chuck E. Cheese comes closest: its group event page publishes a headline price of **$9.99 per child for 30+ kids**, minimum 10 kids, **no deposit required**, bookable up to 12 months ahead. [Operator] ([CEC group events](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/group-events/))

**[Inference]** The growth idea for the work sample: a "bring the whole crew" product with instant per-head pricing that a team parent or a cousin-group organiser can self-serve, sitting between the birthday booking flow and the corporate lead form. It converts a segment that currently falls into a form and waits.

## 6. The fundraiser and community mechanic

This is the strongest outbound wedge available, and the numbers are all publicly posted.

### 6.1 Published donation rates

| Venue | Donation | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Main Event** | **20% of all sales** to the nonprofit ("Spirit Night Fundraiser") | [Operator] ([Main Event schools](https://www.mainevent.com/events/school-events/)) |
| **Chuck E. Cheese** | **20% back** standard; **25% back** if event sales are $2,500+ (promotional, for events booked 3/2/2026 to 4/26/2026 and held by 4/26/2027). **$250 minimum event sales** or 0% donation. | [Operator] ([CEC fundraisers](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/fundraisers/)) |
| **Urban Air** | **20%** of proceeds | [Operator] ([Urban Air school fundraisers](https://www.urbanair.com/group-events/school-fundraisers/)) |
| **Sky Zone** | **20%** of ticket sales from attendees of the event | [Operator] ([Sky Zone fundraising](https://www.skyzone.com/fundraising/)) |
| **Ninja Nation** | **20%** of all tagged sales | [Operator] ([Ninja Nation](https://www.ninjanation.com/spirit-night-fundraiser)) |
| **Stars and Strikes** | **15%** of all purchases excluding alcohol, Mon to Fri | [Operator] ([Stars and Strikes](https://starsandstrikes.com/events/fundraisers/)) |

**20% is the category standard.** Main Event is already at the standard rate.

### 6.2 How they are structured mechanically

From the Chuck E. Cheese program, which is the most fully documented [Operator] ([CEC fundraisers](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/fundraisers/)):
1. **Request** at least 21 days before the event. Reservations team confirms qualifying status (non-profit, public or private school, **at least 75 enrolled students**).
2. **Promote**: the venue supplies flyers, coupons, posters, and printable/emailable invitations. Urban Air additionally supplies pickup-zone signage and **day-of stickers for every child in the school**. [Operator] ([Urban Air](https://www.urbanair.com/group-events/school-fundraisers/))
3. **Attribute**: sales count only when the organisation is **mentioned at the register**, or when a "Fundraiser" item is added to an online order. Self-serve kiosk purchases do **not** count.
4. **Earn**: donation is a percentage of attributed sales including game play, food, drinks and merchandise.
5. **Sweeteners**: free meal for teachers in attendance; a mascot visit to the school between 2 and 4pm on event day.

Chuck E. Cheese explicitly directs organisers to "contact your local **Field Marketing Specialist**" [Operator] ([CEC fundraiser video transcript](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/fundraisers/)). That is the same job as Event Sales Manager outbound, under a different title, and it confirms this is sold locally by a human, not just inbound.

### 6.3 Why it is the best outbound wedge

**[Inference]**, but tightly reasoned from the sourced mechanics above:

1. **It inverts the cold call.** You are not asking a school for money. You are offering to give a school money. That is the only outbound call in this category where the prospect's first reaction is gratitude.
2. **It buys distribution you cannot otherwise buy.** The school itself sends flyers home to every family, puts signage at pickup, and stickers every child. Urban Air's material makes this explicit. A venue cannot legally or practically get that reach any other way.
3. **The economics are defensible.** You pay 20% of *incremental* sales on a *weeknight*, which is the lowest-opportunity-cost inventory in the building. Chuck E. Cheese's **$250 minimum** and Stars and Strikes' **Mon to Fri only** restriction show operators are deliberately protecting peak. [Operator]
4. **It is a warm introduction to an entire community, and the community is the target list.** One fundraiser night puts the sales manager in front of the PTA chair, the principal, the athletic director, the room parents, and 200 to 400 families in one evening. Every one of those is a birthday lead, a team-night lead, or a corporate lead in their day job.
5. **It is inherently annual.** School fundraising is a recurring calendar obligation, which makes it the easiest thing in the pipeline to rebook year over year, which is exactly what the posting asks for.
6. **It seeds the family funnel at scale.** Ties directly back to the D&B 10-K point in section 0: attendees are largely first-time or lapsed guests. [Filing]

### 6.4 The sales sequence this implies

**[Inference]**: fundraiser night (low friction, they say yes) → relationship with the PTA/AD/principal → field trip, sports banquet, and teacher appreciation events → the parents in that community become birthday and season pass buyers → the parents' employers become corporate accounts. One school unlocks four revenue lines.

## 8. What good looks like: four venue group flows worth copying

### 8.1 Chuck E. Cheese group events — the instant-price benchmark

[Operator] ([CEC group events](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/group-events/))

What it does well:
- **Price in the page title and meta description**: "$9.99/Child For 30+ Kids." A price appears before the user clicks anything.
- **Location-gated price reveal**: "Select a location to see package pricing," so a national brand still shows a real local number.
- **No deposit required**, stated up front. Removes the single largest friction point for a volunteer team parent spending money they have not collected yet.
- **Books up to 12 months ahead** and handles the volunteer's real anxiety in the FAQ: "If fewer people show up than booked, what do I pay?"
- **Package told as a list of guarantees, not features**: reserved seat for every kid, all-you-can-play for the full duration, one medium pizza per five kids, bottomless drinks, grab bag so nobody leaves empty handed.
- **Segmented capture form** with an Org Type dropdown (Sports Team / School Group / Day Care / After School Activity / Youth Group) and a distinct "I need help planning, contact me ASAP" checkbox that separates hot leads from newsletter signups.
- **Answers objections nobody asks out loud**: why kids cannot use the prize counter during a group event, and what happens to earned E-Tickets.

### 8.2 Chuck E. Cheese fundraisers — the best turnkey B2B page in the category

[Operator] ([CEC fundraisers](https://www.chuckecheese.com/events/fundraisers/))

- **Tiered donation table published openly**: under $250 = 0%, $250 to $2,499 = 20%, $2,500+ = 25%. It teaches the organiser to drive attendance, because their payout scales.
- **Three-step visual process**: Request, Promote & Invite, Earn.
- **Eligibility stated up front** (non-profit, 75+ students) so unqualified leads self-screen out before they consume sales time.
- **The full FAQ is the contract**: what counts as attributed sales, why kiosks do not count, what happens below minimum.
- **A dated deadline** creates urgency on a product that otherwise has none.
- Named local contact ("Field Marketing Specialist") as an alternative to the form.

### 8.3 Topgolf company events — the hybrid self-serve/assisted split

[Operator] ([Topgolf company events](https://topgolf.com/us/plan-an-event/company-events/))

- **Explicit capacity threshold that routes the user**: book online instantly for up to **24 guests**; above that, an event planner takes over. This is the cleanest published example of a venue deciding where self-serve ends.
- **Per-person starting price published by location** (13+ players from ~$46/person nationally, $42 in Mobile, $64 in El Segundo). Regional honesty rather than one national number.
- Room configurations published by setup type (banquet round, classroom, u-shape, theater, cocktail), which lets a corporate planner picture the event without a call.

### 8.4 Main Event's own group form — strong intent capture, weak price transparency

[Operator] ([Main Event group events](https://www.mainevent.com/events/group-events/))

What it does well:
- Captures **desired date and estimated attendee count** in the form, which is the qualification data a sales manager needs before the first call.
- **"I'd like to receive a free tour of the center"** checkbox: a self-identifying high-intent signal, and a booked site tour is the strongest single stage in a venue pipeline.
- **"I'd need information on a multi-location event"** checkbox: routes national accounts to the right team instead of trapping them at one store.
- **Both email and SMS consent** captured separately with proper disclosure, which enables the fast first reply the data in section 2.4 demands.
- Audience segmented into named occasions on the page itself (Youth Groups, Adult Social Celebrations, Full Facility Buy Out).

Where it is weaker, and where a work sample can visibly improve it: **[Inference]**
- No price appears anywhere on the group events page. Chuck E. Cheese and Topgolf both publish a starting per-head number. Main Event's $14/person food minimum exists on the Level Up page but not where a group buyer lands.
- No date availability signal. The user submits a date into a void.
- The banquet menu is a **PDF download**, which breaks the flow and cannot be filtered by budget or headcount.
- No capacity calculator, despite marketing a 10 to 2,000 range.
- No social proof on the group pages: no client logos, no testimonials, no photos of an actual corporate event.
- No deposit clarity on the group form, though the Level Up page states 50% and 5-day notice.

### 8.5 Composite checklist for the build

Drawn from the four above:
1. A real starting price, per head, visible before any form
2. Location-aware pricing
3. Package comparison side by side rather than as separate pages
4. A capacity/headcount input that changes the price live
5. Date availability shown, not guessed
6. A stated deposit and cancellation policy
7. An explicit threshold where self-serve ends and a human begins
8. Segmented intake that routes by org type and occasion
9. A high-intent signal the user can volunteer (tour request)
10. Objection-handling FAQ written for the actual buyer (a volunteer spending other people's money)
11. Social proof placed on the group page, not only the consumer page

## 9. What I could not verify

Stated plainly so nothing here gets presented as fact.

1. **Which CRM Main Event or Dave & Buster's actually uses.** No public disclosure found. Workday for HR is confirmed; that is a different system. Do not name Salesforce, Tripleseat, or anything else as Main Event's CRM.
2. **What the "sales data warehouse platform" in the posting is.** Not disclosed.
3. **The full verbatim Main Event Brea job posting.** LinkedIn blocked direct fetch. All posting language above comes from aggregator summaries and the parallel D&B on-premise Event Sales Manager listing, and should be re-verified against the live posting before it is quoted in the work sample.
4. **Main Event's current special events share of revenue.** The 9.8% figure is fiscal 2018 for Dave & Buster's, pre-acquisition of Main Event. No current figure found.
5. **What percentage of special event attendees are first-time guests.** D&B says "a significant percentage" in its 10-K but publishes no number.
6. **Any audited conversion rate for FEC group sales specifically.** Every conversion figure in this document comes from restaurant, hotel, or general private-venue sources (primarily Sway and Event Temple, both vendors with a commercial interest). There is no published FEC-specific funnel benchmark I could find. Treat all of section 2.3 as directional.
7. **Sway's sample size and methodology.** They publish medians and pass-through ranges from "our venue portfolio" without disclosing venue count, date range detail, or independent audit. Their venues skew restaurant, bar, gallery and wellness, not FEC.
8. **Membership retention lift.** No published member-vs-non-member visit frequency, churn, or lifetime value data for any FEC loyalty program.
9. **Main Event fundraiser mechanics beyond the 20% headline.** Minimum sales threshold, advance notice requirement, and eligibility rules are not published on mainevent.com the way Chuck E. Cheese publishes them. Do not assume they match Chuck E. Cheese's.
10. **Stars and Strikes fundraiser page** would not render on direct fetch; the 15% figure comes from a search result summary of that page, not the page itself.
11. **Actual Main Event Brea trade area data**: number of schools, youth leagues, corporate employers within a given radius. Not researched here.
12. **Whether Main Event has a subscription membership anywhere.** I found only the seasonal Season Pass, Fun Cards, and Leagues. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
