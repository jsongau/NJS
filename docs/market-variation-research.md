### Five things in this table that will get you asked a follow-up

1. **Sevier is the only non-metro county in your set** — a Micropolitan area. Any screen that filters on "MSA" drops your home market. And its resident median income ($62,581) badly understates the commercial activity there.
2. **Only two counties are meaningfully "rural" in the colloquial sense — and neither actually is.** Fourteen of sixteen are in MSAs. If your model has a "rural" tier, it is empty. Reframe the axis as **drive time to venue**, not urbanicity.
3. **Sullivan and Washington sit in different MSAs but share one DMA.** Montgomery is its own MSA inside the Nashville DMA. MSA and DMA are not interchangeable in this dataset.
4. **Williamson County's $135,594 median income is 2.3x Sullivan's $58,807.** That is the single largest legitimate spread in the table and it maps directly onto a sourced behavioral difference (see §4).
5. **Shelby is the only county shrinking** (910,226 vs. 929,744 in 2020, −2.1%).

### The Sevier drive-time caveat that matters commercially

Sevierville → Pigeon Forge is 6 miles. It is 8–12 minutes at 6am and **45–60 minutes on a peak fall Saturday** because of Parkway congestion ([source](https://www.trippy.com/distance/Sevierville-to-Pigeon-Forge)). Your model's Sevier drive time is not a constant — it is the variable with the widest intra-county variance in the entire dataset. If tour booking is drive-time-sensitive, Sevier's own multiplier should probably be seasonal.

### The addressable-pool statement

Meta's location targeting default is **"Living in or recently in this location."** As of 2024 Meta removed the dropdown that let advertisers select "Living in," "Recently in," or "Travelling in" separately — the combined setting is now effectively the only option ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/new-update-to-meta-ads-location-targeting-404124)). *(Flag: Meta's own help pages are JavaScript-gated and could not be rendered directly; this is relayed from trade press. Verify in Ads Manager before it goes on a slide.)*

**DERIVED — the core arithmetic:**

> Pigeon Forge alone received **12,745,918 visitors in 2023** (city-published). That is **~1,062,000 visitor-trips per month**.
>
> Sevier County has **101,342 residents**.
>
> A 30-day Meta flight geo-targeted to Sevier County under the mandatory "living in or recently in" setting therefore addresses on the order of **1.1 million people, versus roughly 101,000 residents — an inflation factor of approximately 11x.** And that figure is Pigeon Forge alone. It excludes Gatlinburg and Sevierville entirely.

Applying Meta's mandatory 21+ age floor for alcohol scales numerator and denominator in the same direction, so the ~11x ratio is roughly preserved. **ESTIMATE.**

**Cross-check, independent method (DERIVED):** Sevier's $3.93B direct visitor spend ÷ TN's $339 overnight-visitor average = **11.6M visitors**; ÷ $217 blended average = **18.1M visitors**. The spend-implied range of **11.6M–18.1M annual visitors** brackets and exceeds the 12.7M Pigeon Forge count, which is what you'd expect once Gatlinburg and Sevierville are included. The two methods agree. That is the defensible thing to say in the room.

### Four caveats you must carry, or you will get caught

1. **The 11.5M park number is not a Sevier County number and is not people.** NPS counts *recreation visits* from traffic loops × assumed vehicle occupancy, then haircuts 20% (Jun–Aug) and 16% (Sep–May) for duplication. Per NPS's own [counting instructions](https://irma.nps.gov/Stats/FileDownload/33392), **~23% of instrumented entrance traffic comes in from North Carolina and never touches Sevier County.** Sugarlands (the Gatlinburg entrance) is the single busiest counter at ~38% of the instrumented set, with Gatlinburg's two counters together ~44%. Building reach on 11.5M overstates the Sevier audience substantially.
2. **No published Gatlinburg visitor count exists.** I checked the CVB, the city, and TDTD. The CVB's site says Gatlinburg is "a basecamp for many of the 14 million park visitors" — a stale, promotional figure matching no NPS year since 2021. **Do not put a Gatlinburg visitor number in the model.**
3. **No published average length of stay exists** for Sevier County, Gatlinburg, or Pigeon Forge. Any "3 to 4 nights" figure you find is unsourced blog content.
4. **Tourists are a transient pool, not a retargetable one.** They are addressable for the days they are in-market and then they go home to a different DMA. Retargeting pools built in Sevier decay fast, and lookalike seeds built from Sevier converters will be geographically scattered. This is the single most important operational consequence of the 11x number, and it cuts *against* a naive "Sevier is 11x bigger so spend 11x there" conclusion.

## 3. Narrow vs. broad vs. retargeting — the findings

### 3a. The strongest argument for broad is arithmetic, not vendor claims

Meta's documented learning-phase threshold is **~50 optimization events per ad set per rolling 7 days** ([Meta Business Help 112167992830700](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700) — page is JS-gated; figure is consistently reported across independent sources). The 50 events are **per ad set, not per ad**. Splitting a budget into five interest-stacked ad sets divides one conversion pool five ways.

**DERIVED:** at a $30 CPA you need ~$1,500/week (~$214/day) *per ad set* to exit learning; at $100 CPA, ~$5,000/week (~$714/day). Below that, both strategies starve — but narrow starves worse, because it also removes the algorithm's ability to find volume. **The correct small-budget/small-geo conclusion is "consolidate to one broad ad set," not "narrow works better."**

Separately, Meta's own guidance recommends audiences of **2–10 million** and states that low audience size "can cause your cost per result to be at least twice as much" ([Meta Business Help 321695409726523](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/321695409726523) — relayed via snippet, page not renderable).

**This is the finding that should reshape your model.** A rural DMA plus a niche whiskey interest stack is a *double* thin-audience problem. The cost increase you'd observe is attributable to audience thinness, not to rurality — and the fix is to broaden the interest layer, not the geography. A geography-based CPM multiplier would point you at the wrong lever.

No Meta-published methodology document exists for any of these. That absence is itself the finding.

### 3c. The strongest independent counter-evidence: Haus, 640 Meta experiments

[haus.io/blog/the-meta-report](https://www.haus.io/blog/the-meta-report-lessons-from-640-haus-incrementality-experiments) · [haus.io/blog/is-meta-incremental](https://www.haus.io/blog/is-meta-incremental)

640 Meta incrementality experiments since 2024. **Sample caveat: Haus sells incrementality testing, and its clients average $14M/yr in Meta spend — top of market, not comparable to a regional spirits brand's budget.**

- Meta drove **~19% average lift** to primary KPI across the sample.
- **58% of brands saw higher iROAS on Manual campaigns than Advantage+.** On average Advantage+ delivered **12% lower DTC iROAS at 18% lower daily spend**.
- Advantage+ led by +9% at experiment midpoint, then fell behind. Post-window lift: **+17% Advantage+ vs +32% Manual.**
- **Advantage+ over-reports by 12 percentage points relative to Manual.** Haus's read: the intent model works *too* well — it finds people who would have bought anyway.
- Counterweight: Advantage+ drove a **higher new-customer share (70% vs 65%)** and higher omnichannel halo (+51% vs +43%).
- **Do not split the difference:** brands moving from a >75%-skewed setup to 50/50 saw an **18% drop in iROAS**.
- Retargeting was **~15% of Meta spend** in both Advantage+ and Manual.
- Lower-volume brands (<5,000 purchase events/week) saw only 5% lower DTC efficiency from mid-funnel optimization — i.e. thin accounts gain from moving *up* the funnel to feed the algorithm, not from narrowing.

### 3d. A clean, small, honest A/B test

Jon Loomer, 30 days, $25/day per cell, ~$2,250 total ([writeup](https://www.jonloomer.com/test-results-advantage-plus-audience-detailed-targeting-lookalikes/)): Advantage+ Audience CPM was $0.68 below Lookalikes and $0.89 below Detailed Targeting; it produced 9 more registrations than Detailed Targeting and 36 more than Lookalikes. **But he reports the Advantage+ vs. Detailed Targeting gap was not significant — Meta estimated a 36% chance Detailed Targeting wins on a rerun.** The win over Lookalikes was significant.

**The structural fact buried in that post, and it's the most important one in this whole section:** when optimizing for conversions, **Advantage Detailed Targeting and Advantage Lookalike are force-enabled and cannot be switched off.** "Narrow" is no longer actually narrow. Any model that treats interest targeting as a hard constraint is modeling a product Meta no longer sells.

### 3e. Retargeting vs. prospecting — the benchmarks are not real

**I could not find a single credible, methodologically transparent public benchmark for retargeting vs. prospecting CVR/CPA.** The circulating figures (retargeting ROAS 4.2x vs prospecting 2.2x; retargeting CVR 3.8% vs 1.5%; CPA 30–50% lower) appear across 2025–2026 SEO blogs with no stated sample size, period, or source, several of them AI-generated and citing each other. **Do not put them in a deck.**

What *is* defensible: retargeting always looks better on platform-reported metrics in every source — and the incrementality literature says that is exactly what **selection** produces, not causation. The gap is the artifact, not the insight.

### 3f. The incrementality evidence — real numbers

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (2015)**, *Econometrica* 83(1) — [PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/stadelis/BNT_ECMA_rev.pdf) | eBay paid-search RCT | Observational ROI **+4,100%** (no controls) / **+1,400%** (with controls); **experimental ROI −63%**. Confidence interval excludes break-even. **99.5% of paid brand-term clicks were recaptured by organic search.** Frequent users, whose purchasing was unaffected, absorbed most of the spend — structurally identical to what retargeting does. |
| **Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky (2019)**, *Marketing Science* 38(2) — [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/causality/2019-gordon.pdf) | **15 Facebook RCTs, 500M user-experiment obs, 1.6B impressions** | "In half our studies the estimated percentage increase in purchase outcomes is **off by a factor of three**." Study 1: naive lift 217%, corrected 93.6%, **true RCT 30%**. Study 9: **true RCT 2.4%** vs best observational estimate **1,306%**. Bias is worst at the purchase end of the funnel. |
| **Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer (2022)**, *Marketing Science* — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.07055) | **663 Facebook RCTs, 5,000+ user-level features** | Median true RCT lift **29% upper / 18% mid / 5% lower funnel**. Double-ML estimates: 83% / 58% / **24%**. Stratified PSM: 173% / 176% / **64%**. **At the lower funnel — where retargeting lives — DML overstates ~4.8x and SPSM ~12.8x.** Conclusion verbatim: "we are unable to reliably estimate an ad campaign's causal effect." |
| **Lewis & Rao (2015)**, *QJE* 130(4) — [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/advertising/2015-lewis.pdf) | 25 large field experiments, $2.8M spend, millions of customers | **Median ROI confidence interval was over 100 percentage points wide.** Informative experiments "can easily require more than 10 million person-weeks." **If a multi-million-customer RCT can't tell +50% from −50% ROI, a regional brand's two-week holdout is measuring noise.** |
| **Sahni, Narayanan & Kalyanam (2019)**, *JMR* 56(3) — [link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022243718813987) | Retargeting RCT, online retailer | Retargeting **did** cause **14.6% more users to return to site within 4 weeks**; 33% of week-1 effect landed on day 1. So retargeting is incremental — but ~15% on a site-return metric, not the 3–5x conversion multiple platform reporting implies. |
| **Lambrecht & Tucker (2013)**, *JMR* 50(5) — [link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.11.0503) | Dynamic vs. generic retargeting | **Dynamic product-specific retargeted ads were on average LESS effective than generic equivalents**, except once browsing showed preferences had firmed up. The industry default is the wrong default for most of the funnel. |

**The rigorous version of "Meta grades its own homework":** Braun & Schwartz, *Where A-B Testing Goes Wrong*, *Journal of Marketing* ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3896024)). **Divergent delivery** — platform experiment tools deliver each arm to a different, algorithmically-optimized, unobservable mix of users, so exposure within the test is not random. They show this can **flip the magnitude and even the sign** of A/B results, and that PSA/placebo controls don't fix it. Meta's own researchers have conceded the problem ([arXiv 2508.21251](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.21251)).

**Meta Conversion Lift limits:** minimum 7-day test, ≥10% of audience per cell (per Haus; not verifiable against Meta's gated pages). It only measures conversions Meta can see — Haus found **~32% of Meta's impact for omnichannel brands landed on non-DTC channels** (Amazon, retail), invisible to Conversion Lift, meaning **the average omnichannel brand needs only 0.68x DTC iROAS to break even overall.** That is directly relevant to Ole Smoky, which sells through distribution and retail, not primarily DTC. Note also the bias runs both ways: Haus found Meta *under*-reports by ~15% for click-only advertisers on 7-day-click DTC.

**Geo-holdout:** [Meta GeoLift](https://github.com/facebookincubator/GeoLift) is open source and, per an [independent simulation study by Recast](https://research.getrecast.com/geolift-sim-study), beat CausalPy, Google Matched Markets, and CausalImpact on 3 of 4 metrics (92–95% coverage, 3–5% false positive rate). **The catch: its confidence intervals are frequently wide enough to contain both the true effect and zero simultaneously.** Say that before selling anyone a geo test.

### 3g. Does Meta CPM differ by geography? The honest answer is the one you suspected

**No vendor publishes a US state-level or DMA-level Meta CPM table.** I checked Gupta Media, Varos, Revealbot, WordStream/LocaliQ, Superads, Emplifi, Triple Whale, Lebesgue. Every benchmark product stops at country, industry, objective, or placement. The city/state CPC figures circulating in SEO content ("New York $2.10, Alabama $1.35") have **no stated methodology, sample size, or date**, and several of those pages are AI-generated. **They are directionally plausible and evidentially worthless.**

**The mechanism, from Meta's own developer documentation** ([Marketing API bidding overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/bidding/overview)):

> "Facebook evaluates your bid strategy, bid_amount, the probability of acquiring your optimization_goal to calculate an effective bid so that you only win auctions and have your ads delivered when you are likely to reach your optimization_goal at a certain bidding constraints such as cost per result."

**There is no geographic price input.** Location is a targeting filter, not a pricing input.

**Peer-reviewed proof that Meta prices the *same* targeted audience differently by predicted relevance:** Ali et al., *Discrimination through optimization*, [CSCW 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02095) — skewed delivery occurs "due to market and financial optimization effects as well as the platform's own predictions about the 'relevance' of ads to different groups of users." Ali et al., [WSDM 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04255) — Facebook's delivery algorithms "effectively differentiate the price of reaching a user based on their inferred political alignment with the advertised content."

**Say this in the interview:** *"Meta prices by auction competition and predicted action rate, not by geography. Its own API docs contain no geographic price input. But geography correlates with competition density, audience commercial value, and audience thinness — so observed CPMs do differ. There is no published dataset that quantifies it, so I did not build a geo CPM multiplier I couldn't source. I'd measure it in-account instead: DMA is a native Meta targeting primitive (`location_types=["geo_market"]`, e.g. `DMA:501 New York` — [API docs](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/reference/targeting-search/)) and it's a standard Ads Manager breakdown."*

**The one geography fact that IS actionable and is stronger than any CPM table:** budget flows to the cheapest CPM *inside an ad set*. Put Nashville and rural East Tennessee in one ad set and delivery will collapse into whichever is cheaper, starving the other. **That is a structural argument for splitting geographies into separate ad sets** — and it also collides with the 50-conversions-per-ad-set constraint in §3a. Naming that tension is the single best thing you can do in this interview.

**Seasonality (the one geo-adjacent multiplier that IS sourceable)** — [Gupta Media](https://www.guptamedia.com/social-media-ads-cost), a single consistent panel: Meta Q4 premium vs Q1–Q3 was **+13% (2022) and +17% (2023)**. Cyber Monday 2024 CPM $17.70 = **+138% vs the 2024 annualized $7.43**. **Use a Q4 quarterly multiplier of 1.15–1.20x and a separate day-level BFCM spike of 2.0–2.4x.** Do not use 138% as a quarterly multiplier — it would inflate the quarter ~7x. Day-of-week variation is trivial (~9% spread); don't model it.

**Benchmark warning:** for the same country and period, Gupta reports $8.19 CPM, Varos $22.20, Superads $23.42. **A ~3x disagreement, entirely driven by whose clients are in the panel** (Gupta skews entertainment/awareness; Varos and Superads skew DTC ecommerce conversion). **Do not average them.**

### 3h. Alcohol targeting — what exists, and why it barely matters now

Third-party interest sizes ([AdTargeting](https://adtargeting.io)): Cocktail 107.7M, Whisky 84.0M, Drinking 31.1M, Distillation 28.7M, Jack Daniel's Tennessee Whiskey 9.6M, Blended whiskey 3.9M, Malt whisky 1.6M, Buffalo Trace Distillery 1.05M.

**⚠️ Three caveats that gut these numbers:**
1. **They are almost certainly GLOBAL, not US.** A "Whisky" interest at 84M against a US 21+ Facebook reach of ~150–200M implies implausible penetration. **ESTIMATE: US share is likely 15–30%.** Re-pull with `country=US` in Ads Manager before using.
2. **No public source lists the numeric interest IDs.** Meta's own docs say to validate by ID, not name, because "interests may be renamed at any time."
3. **No published figures exist at all for "Bourbon whiskey," "Moonshine," "Tennessee whiskey," "Bartending," or "Distillery tours."**

**Targeting deprecation is broader than the exclusions removal you already know about.** Exclusions announced 21 Jan 2025, removed from ad sets 31 Mar 2025 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-removes-detailed-targeting-exclusions-for-ad-accounts/), [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-removes-detailed-targeting-exclusions-from-ad-campaigns/723389/)). But Meta also began **consolidating detailed interest categories into broader groupings on 23 Jun 2025** ([Brandwatch](https://social-media-management-help.brandwatch.com/en/articles/13215856-meta-changes-to-detailed-targeting-interests-in-advertise)), and Advantage+ became the default for new campaigns around Feb 2026 with detailed targeting functioning as **suggestions rather than hard constraints**. Meta's API exposes a formal deprecation lifecycle (`NORMAL`, `NON-DELIVERABLE`, `DEPRECATING`) queryable via `type=targetingoptionstatus` — **run that against your whiskey shortlist before building a plan on it.**

**Compliance, and this one is non-negotiable for an alcohol client.** Meta requires alcohol ads to "include age and country targeting criteria consistent with Meta's targeting requirements and applicable local laws" ([Meta Transparency Report to IARD, Apr 2024, PDF](https://iard.org/IARD/media/Actions-documents/140414Meta-Transparency-Report.pdf) — *note: verify this URL, my source path was `140414`/`140414` variant*). US floor is 21+. Practitioner reports conflict on whether Advantage+ can override age: one source says location and minimum age are treated as strict rules while gender, maximum age, and interests are overridable. **The safe posture: hard-code the 21+ floor at ad set level in every ad set, and never use interest targeting as a compliance control** — interests are explicitly overridable and exclusions no longer exist.

**Bottom line for your model: an interest-stack media model has weak predictive value in 2026.** Build on **geography + hard 21+ age floor + creative**, with interests as a soft seed layer only. Saying that unprompted is a strong signal.

### Brand awareness decay with distance — the honest answer

**There is no published study measuring brand awareness decay by distance from home market for spirits, whiskey, or alcohol. I could not verify one exists. Do not claim one does.**

The nearest real evidence:

- **Bronnenberg, Dhar & Dubé (2009), "Brand History, Geography, and the Persistence of Brand Shares," *Journal of Political Economy* 117(1)** — [PDF](https://sites.duke.edu/collardwexler/files/2015/01/bronnenberg_dhar_dube.pdf). 34 CPG categories across the 50 largest US cities, matched to each brand's historic launch city. **Both market share and consumer perceived quality are higher near a brand's city of origin and decline with distance**, persisting for decades. *Caveat precisely: the outcomes are market share and perceived quality, not survey-measured awareness.*
- **Bronnenberg, Dubé & Gentzkow (2012), *AER* 102(6)** — [PDF](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/brands.pdf). Using consumer migration to isolate causation: brand preferences formed in a prior residence persist **up to 50 years** and explain **~40% of geographic variation in market shares**.
- **Functional form.** Huff (1963) gravity model, *Land Economics* 39(1): P(ij) = (S_j / T_ij^λ) / Σ(S_k / T_ik^λ), with **λ typically 1.5–2 in applied retail work** ([GISGeography](https://gisgeography.com/huff-gravity-model/), [Esri](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/3.5/tool-reference/business-analyst/understanding-huff-model.htm)). Rosselló-Nadal & Santana-Gallego (2022), [*Journal of Economic Surveys*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joes.12502): meta-review of **143 tourism studies, 104 distance elasticities, 96% significantly negative, mean ≈ −1.0, median ≈ −0.83.** Chen (2015), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02915): **use power law, not exponential, for large multi-scale regional systems.**
- **Beverage-adjacent:** Nilsson (2019), [*Growth and Change*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/grow.12292) — Denver home-price premiums up to **20.4%** near a brewpub, **declining with distance**, shifting beyond ~0.5 km. A genuine quantified local-halo decay for a place-based beverage brand.
- **No peer-reviewed distance-decay analysis of distillery visitation exists** that I could verify. The best real datapoint on distillery catchment: **63% of non-local Tennessee distillery visits are overnight, 37% daytrip** (Tourism Economics, 2022) — most non-local distillery traffic is already travelling far enough to need a hotel.

**Say this if asked:** *"There's no published study on spirits brand awareness decaying with distance. The nearest real evidence is Bronnenberg, Dhar and Dubé in the JPE, which shows CPG brand share and perceived quality both decline with distance from origin and persist for decades. For a curve, retail gravity and tourism demand both point to power-law decay with an exponent around 1 to 2. I'm importing that assumption and labeling it as imported, not measured."*

## 5. Recommended geographic multipliers

### How they are built — state this before showing the numbers

**Baseline = a 60-minute drive at the national median drinking incidence (54%).** All three multipliers are relative to that reference point.

| Multiplier | Formula | Sourced to |
|---|---|---|
| **Entry rate** | income-drinker factor × (60 / max(t,15))^**0.20** | [Gallup 2025 income bands](https://news.gallup.com/poll/693362/drinking-rate-new-low-alcohol-concerns-surge.aspx) (39/54/66%) × a deliberately shallow decay, because ad-driven click behavior is far less distance-constrained than physical visitation |
| **Tour booking** | (60 / max(t,15))^**0.83** | [Rosselló-Nadal & Santana-Gallego 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joes.12502): median distance elasticity **−0.83** across 104 published tourism-demand estimates. This is the most robust empirical regularity in tourism economics. |
| **Brand-buyer share** | (60 / max(t,15))^**0.35** | [Bronnenberg, Dhar & Dubé (JPE 2009)](https://sites.duke.edu/collardwexler/files/2015/01/bronnenberg_dhar_dube.pdf) — direction and existence of decay are sourced. **The exponent 0.35 is an ESTIMATE**, chosen shallower than the tourism curve because Ole Smoky is nationally distributed and seeds awareness remotely via ~5M annual venue visitors who then go home. |

The **15-minute floor** prevents the curve exploding at zero distance. **Income → drinker incidence** is linearly interpolated across Gallup's band midpoints ($25K→39%, $70K→54%, $140K→66%) and indexed to the national 54%. That interpolation is a **DERIVED** step.

### Sevier's split-audience treatment — the sophisticated version

Do not model Sevier as one audience. Model it as two:

| Sub-audience | Size (30-day flight) | Entry × | Tour booking × |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Residents** | ~101,342 | **1.26** | **3.16** |
| **In-market travellers** ("recently in this location") | **~1,060,000/mo, Pigeon Forge alone — DERIVED** | **ESTIMATE, needs testing** | **ESTIMATE, needs testing** |

**The addressable pool is ~11x the resident population** — that is the number, and it is DERIVED from a city-published visitor count, cross-checked against a spend-implied 11.6M–18.1M annual visitors. Whether the traveller sub-audience *converts* at a higher rate is an **empirical question I cannot source**, and the honest move is to make it a scenario input with a labeled range rather than a fabricated constant. That is the honest answer and it is also the better plan.
