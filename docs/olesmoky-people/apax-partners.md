## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

The classification matters more than it looks. Apax filed Ole Smoky under Internet and Consumer, not under a food and beverage or industrials heading. That is a firm that thinks about this asset in terms of direct consumer relationships, digital channels and data. A CRM work sample is directly on that thesis, and should say so explicitly rather than assume it is understood.

The reading frame is exit value. A sponsor that took control in March 2022 is now in the part of the hold where operating improvements need to be visible in the numbers and defensible in a data room. Three things translate a loyalty build into that language. First, a consented first party member base is a transferable asset that survives a change of control. Second, receipt capture generates off premise purchase data that three tier law otherwise hides from the brand, which means the company can prove things about its own consumer that competitors cannot prove about theirs. Third, an owned channel reduces dependence on paid media at a moment when acquisition costs are the main variable cost in the marketing line.

The failure mode with a sponsor reader is length and abstraction. Anything that needs three paragraphs of setup before the number appears will be skimmed. Lead with the asset, the cost, the payback, and the risk register. Then show the build.

## What makes them say no

- Capital intensity. A programme that needs a platform purchase, a data warehouse project and three new hires before it produces a single record is a no at first sight.
- A payback horizon that runs past a plausible exit.
- Regulatory exposure that would have to be disclosed. Georgia and Alabama both prohibit loyalty points on alcohol purchases outright, Ga. Comp. R. and Regs. 560-2-2-.14(1) and Ala. Admin. Code 20-X-6-.12. A programme that ignores that is a diligence problem, not a marketing problem.
- Unlabelled assumptions. A model built on derived numbers presented as measured ones fails the first hour of diligence.
- Anything that reads as a brand vanity project rather than an operating lever.
