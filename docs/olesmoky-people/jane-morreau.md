## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

If she is still involved, or if her successor on the board is cut from similar cloth, this is the reader who checks the arithmetic. A CFO of a Brown-Forman scale spirits company has seen the inside of the three tier economics: gross margin by channel, distributor programming spend, the difference between depletions and shipments, and how quickly a marketing programme's cost per acquisition drifts once it leaves the pilot. She will not be impressed by a large addressable number. She will ask what the incremental margin is on a redeemed reward and who books the liability.

That last point is the one most loyalty work samples miss, and the one that would most impress her. Points are a liability. A programme that issues a $15 credit at 2,500 points and a 20% coupon at 2,500 points, stacking, applied to the subtotal first, has created a deferred obligation that has to be estimated, accrued and reported. Showing the breakage assumption and the accrual treatment, even roughly, signals that the person building this understands it is a balance sheet item and not just a marketing feature.

The second thing she would test is the revenue attribution. The evidence base here already says tastings are a rebated entry fee rather than revenue, because a $5 Gatlinburg tasting includes a $5 off coupon and a $10 Nashville tasting includes a $10 off coupon. Booking tasting revenue at zero is the honest treatment and it is the treatment a former CFO would arrive at independently. Doing it before she asks is worth more than any interface.

## What makes them say no

- Points issued with no liability or breakage treatment.
- Revenue lines that double count. Booking a rebated tasting fee as revenue is the specific trap here.
- Gross numbers without margin. Redemption volume means nothing without the cost of what is redeemed.
- Any model whose sensitivity to its weakest assumption is not shown. The 1.2% link click through planning figure in the media model is derived and untraceable, and should be labelled as such rather than buried.
- Vendor revenue estimates for a private company. They disagree by roughly 25% and using one as fact is a tell.
