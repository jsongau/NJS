# Joe Baker
**Co-founder, Ole Smoky Distillery** · founded 2010 · previously a criminal defence lawyer in East Tennessee, University of Tennessee College of Law

## Confirmed

- Co-founded Ole Smoky in the Smoky Mountains of East Tennessee with Cory Cottongim and Tony Breeden. The distillery launched on 4 July 2010 in Gatlinburg. Source: https://www.distillerytrail.com/blog/americas-no-1-most-visited-distillery-ole-smoky-distillery-has-been-acquired/ and https://alumni.utk.edu/joe-baker-01-and-jessi-baker-03/.
- His wife Jessi Edwards Baker was part of the founding group, alongside partners Cory Cottongim, Tony Breeden and Chuck Edwards. Source: https://alumni.utk.edu/joe-baker-01-and-jessi-baker-03/.
- Trained as a lawyer. Undergraduate at Georgetown, law degree at the University of Tennessee. Practised criminal law before founding the distillery. Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/amyguttman/2014/12/23/criminal-lawyer-cashes-in-on-moonshine/ (23 December 2014) and https://law.utk.edu/2016/11/13/volunteer-spirits/ (13 November 2016).
- The business started because Tennessee legalised moonshine production in 2009. Baker took family recipes commercial the following year. Source: https://law.utk.edu/2016/11/13/volunteer-spirits/ (13 November 2016).
- Deliberately sited the first distillery in Gatlinburg to capture tourist traffic to the Great Smoky Mountains National Park. Source: https://www.forbes.com/sites/amyguttman/2014/12/23/criminal-lawyer-cashes-in-on-moonshine/ (23 December 2014).
- Remains a significant shareholder after the 2022 Apax transaction, alongside Cory Cottongim and management. Source: https://www.apax.com/news-views/apax-funds-to-acquire-controlling-stake-in-ole-smoky-distillery-from-centerview-capita/ (11 February 2022).
- Lists himself as Founder at Ole Smoky Distillery. Source: https://www.linkedin.com/in/joebakerlaw/.

## What they say they care about

"We present an authentic product in a fun setting that embraces the history and the culture of the area. I think people really bought into that."
Source: https://www.whiskeylore.org/interviews/whiskeys-history-at-tennessee-and-the-world-s-1-most-visited-distillery.

"In 2010, we opened the first location in Gatlinburg and quickly became the most visited distillery in the world. We have over four million visitors a year and it's fun to be able to share a piece of your hometown with the rest of the world."
Source: https://www.whiskeylore.org/interviews/whiskeys-history-at-tennessee-and-the-world-s-1-most-visited-distillery.

Two things carry across every interview he has given: **authenticity tied to a specific place** and **the visitor standing in front of you**. He built a tasting room business, not a brand marketing business. Foot traffic is the thing he counts.

## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

He is the founder who built the visitor experience, so he is the one person in the room who has watched five million people a year walk through the door and leave without a name attached. Framed that way, a CRM programme is not an abstraction to him. It is the missing half of the thing he already built. The single sentence that will land with him is that the Hooch Hop passport, live since October 2022 across three East Tennessee venues, is a paper booklet with rubber stamps and captures nothing. He knows the passport works as an experience. Showing him a version that keeps the stamp ritual and adds an identified record is an argument for extending his idea, not replacing it.

He is a lawyer, and a criminal one, which changes how he reads risk. He will not be charmed by a mechanic. He will ask what happens when a state regulator looks at it. The compliance work in the sample is not a footnote for him, it is a credential. Naming Tennessee as the hardest state in the plan, citing TABC Rule 0100-01-.03(14)(c) on alcohol as a prize and TCA 47-18-120(c)(3)(C) on publicity releases, and showing an earn on alcohol and redeem on non alcohol architecture that clears Georgia and Alabama, does more for credibility with him than any interface will.

Where he will resist is tone. Ole Smoky's equity is Appalachian, family, place. If the loyalty programme reads like a generic points app with mountain graphics bolted on, he will feel it immediately and he will be right. The Jar Club language work matters specifically for him: quest names that state the action, badge names that keep the romance, nothing borrowed from a competitor's trademark, nothing that sounds like it was written in San Francisco.

## What makes them say no

- Anything that treats the distillery visit as a transaction rather than a hosted experience.
- Loyalty language that could have belonged to any brand. Generic gamification vocabulary on a heritage brand reads as disrespect.
- Legal exposure presented as a detail to be worked out later. He is a lawyer and will hear that as unfinished work.
- Replacing the Hooch Hop passport rather than extending it. The paper stamp is a thing people love. Digitising it away is a loss.
- Overclaiming visitor numbers. The company stopped publishing visitor figures after April 2024 and the trend was down, roughly 5.7 million in 2021 to 5.0 million plus in 2023. Quoting a stale peak figure to a founder who knows the real number is a credibility hit.
