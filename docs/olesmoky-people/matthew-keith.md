What is verifiable is the shape of the job. He owns the wholesale and distributor relationship globally, which in the United States means Southern Glazer's Wine and Spirits among others, and he owns it at a moment when 2024 volume fell 6% to 1.2 million nine litre cases.

## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

He is the stakeholder most likely to kill a consumer CRM programme quietly, and the one most work samples forget exists. The job description for Director, CRM explicitly puts trade and distributor relationships inside the role's scope, which means whoever takes the job will be building things that touch his accounts. If the work sample does not address the three tier structure directly, he will read it as a marketing department building a shadow sales channel.

The way to win him is to hand him something he cannot get himself. Under three tier law, Ole Smoky sells to distributors and never sees who buys the bottle at retail. Receipt capture inside a loyalty programme produces off premise purchase data at account level and SKU level, which is exactly the ammunition a sales leader takes into a distributor review. Framed as "this programme tells you which SKUs move in which accounts and which consumers repeat", the CRM build becomes a sales tool rather than a competing channel. That reframe is the single highest value thing to put in front of him.

The second thing he will test is whether the programme respects tied house rules. Anything that looks like the brand giving something of value to a retailer, or steering consumers to specific accounts in a way that reads as an inducement, is a 27 CFR Part 6 conversation. The useful nuance is that section 6.3(a) limits Part 6 to transactions between industry members and retailers, so a brand direct consumer promotion sits largely outside it, and section 6.96(b) expressly permits direct consumer contest prizes. Knowing that distinction, and being able to say it in one sentence, is what separates someone who has worked in spirits from someone who has read about it.

## What makes them say no

- Any programme that reads as going around the distributor.
- Retailer specific rewards or steering that a distributor could construe as an inducement.
- Consumer data captured and then never shared back with sales in a usable form.
- A programme that adds field team workload without adding depletions.
- Anything premised on direct to consumer spirits shipping in Tennessee. There is no distilled spirits DTC licence in Tennessee. TABC's licence list contains exactly one direct shipper licence and it is Winery Direct Shipper. Proposing spirits DTC in the home state marks the author as an outsider immediately.
