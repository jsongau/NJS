# Michael Novy
**Chief Executive Officer, Ole Smoky Distillery and Tanteo Tequila** · appointed effective 26 August 2025 · previously CEO of Calabasas Beverage Company (818 Tequila, Sprinter), President of Casa Dragones, GM of Constellation's spirits business, 17 years at E&J Gallo

## Confirmed

- Appointed CEO by the Ole Smoky board, announced 26 August 2025, effective immediately. Succeeds Robert Hall, CEO since 2016. - 35 years in the beverage industry. Started at E&J Gallo as a sales representative, spent nearly 17 years there, rising to vice president of marketing and general management roles. Source: https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2025/08/ole-smoky-names-michael-novy-ceo/ (27 August 2025).
- At Constellation Brands he was division vice president and worked closely with Southern Glazer's Wine and Spirits, one of Ole Smoky's distributor partners. Promoted to general manager of Constellation's spirits business, where he drove SVEDKA vodka from a 4% decline to 8% growth. ## What they say they care about

"I'm honored to join Ole Smoky, a deeply authentic brand known for its extraordinary innovation and consumer engagement. I look forward to working with our talented team and industry partners to drive Ole Smoky and Tanteo to new levels of growth in the U.S. and around the world."
Source: https://www.themountainpress.com/business/ole-smoky-names-novy-as-new-ceo-after-halls-retirement/article_46d84db4-2224-503c-b458-e2cdb2016757.html (26 August 2025).

Two words in that sentence do most of the work. **Consumer engagement** is the thing he singled out about the brand he was taking over. **Industry partners** is a distributor reference, and it is not decoration: his Constellation years were spent inside the Southern Glazer's relationship that Ole Smoky depends on.

## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

He reads it as a volume problem first. He arrived at a brand shipping 1.2 million cases and falling 6%. Everything in a CRM pitch has to connect back to depletions, velocity and new household acquisition, not to engagement metrics for their own sake. A work sample that opens with points, badges and tier mechanics will read to him as an app someone wanted to build. A work sample that opens with "five million distillery visits a year produce zero identified customer records, and here is what one identified record is worth in repeat purchase" reads as a growth argument he has made himself at three other companies.

His 818 and Sprinter years matter more than his Gallo years for this. He has just come from businesses where the consumer relationship was direct, fast moving and built on brand affinity rather than shelf position, and where DTC and social were the growth engine. He will be more receptive to first party data as a strategy than a career three tier operator would be. He will also be quicker to spot when a loyalty concept has been copied from a DTC brand that had no three tier constraints. The single strongest item to put in front of him is the receipt upload mechanic: because of three tier law, Ole Smoky cannot see who buys a bottle at Total Wine or through Instacart, and a member who uploads that receipt hands the company off premise purchase proof that no data vendor sells. That is a category level asymmetry, and it is the kind of argument a Constellation GM recognises instantly.

Where he will push back hardest is on the distributor question. He worked Southern Glazer's from the inside. If a CRM programme touches trade partners, on premise activation, or anything a distributor might read as the brand going around them, he will want that named and handled before he hears about the consumer side. The job description explicitly includes trade and distributor relationships in scope, so a work sample that treats CRM as consumer only is answering a narrower question than the one that was asked.

## What makes them say no

- Engagement metrics with no line to volume. Points issued, badges earned, sessions per member. He will ask what happened to depletions.
- Anything that looks like an app project rather than a commercial programme. If the first three slides are UI, he has already decided.
- Ignoring the distributor. A programme that quietly disintermediates Southern Glazer's without saying so is a relationship risk he personally owns.
- Compliance handwaving. He has run restricted category marketing for 35 years. A sweepstakes or loyalty mechanic that would not clear a TTB or state review makes the whole sample look naive.
- Copying a DTC playbook wholesale. He has just come from 818 and Sprinter and knows exactly which parts of that playbook do not survive contact with a three tier spirits business.
