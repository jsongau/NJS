## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

He reads it as an asset value question. Apax took control in March 2022. A private equity hold period of four to six years puts an exit conversation somewhere in the next two to three years, so anything proposed now has to show a return inside that window or it is somebody else's project. This is the reader for whom the payback maths is the deliverable. Cost to stand up, cost to run, incremental revenue per identified member, time to breakeven, and the honest sensitivity around the assumptions that are grade C or D rather than measured.

His Operational Excellence background means he has spent his career inside portfolio companies looking for the operating lever nobody pulled. A first party customer database at a company with five million annual visits and zero captured records is exactly the shape of finding he is paid to notice. He will also see the second order value immediately: a proprietary consumer dataset is a diligence asset at exit. A buyer paying for a spirits brand with a named, consented, purchase attributed member base is paying a different multiple than one buying wholesale depletions alone. Say that out loud. It is the argument that turns a marketing programme into an equity story.

He is the least likely of anyone in this group to be moved by craft, place or brand romance, and the most likely to ask what the same money would earn in media instead. That comparison should be pre empted rather than avoided. Median Meta ROAS across a 20,000 account DTC benchmark is 1.93x, and median incremental ROAS measured against geo holdouts is 2.16x, with 64% of incremental conversions coming from new or reactivated customers. A loyalty build has to be argued against that alternative use of the dollar, not in isolation from it.

## What makes them say no

- No payback period. If the sample cannot say when the money comes back, it is a hobby.
- A return that lands outside the likely hold window.
- Assumptions presented as facts. He does diligence for a living and an unlabelled derived number is the fastest way to lose him. The 47.5% sweepstakes conversion figure is the live example: real, correctly quoted from Unbounce, and wrong for paid social by roughly 3.3x, where the correct floor is 14.3%.
- Anything requiring significant capital expenditure or headcount before it proves out.
- Regulatory exposure that could sit on a disclosure schedule at exit. A sweepstakes mechanic that trips North Carolina 14-306.4 on entertaining displays is a felony exposure question, not a design preference.
