# Robert Hall
**Chief Executive Officer, Ole Smoky Distillery, 2016 to August 2025** · retiring · Chair of Responsibility.org · four decades in food and beverage

## Confirmed

- CEO of Ole Smoky from 2016 until Michael Novy's appointment took effect on 26 August 2025. Nine and a half years in the post. Source: https://www.themountainpress.com/business/ole-smoky-names-novy-as-new-ceo-after-halls-retirement/article_46d84db4-2224-503c-b458-e2cdb2016757.html (26 August 2025).
- Remained with the company for a transition period, then retiring and moving closer to family in California. Source: https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2025/08/ole-smoky-names-michael-novy-ceo/ (27 August 2025).
- Named Chair of Responsibility.org, the alcohol industry's responsible consumption foundation, which also adopted a new strategic plan under him. Source: https://olesmoky.com/blogs/news/responsibility-org-announces-robert-hall-as-new-chair-and-adopts-new-strategic-plan.
- Over four decades of professional experience in the food and beverage industry. Source: https://theorg.com/org/ole-smoky-distillery/org-chart/j-robert-hall.
- Led Ole Smoky through the Centerview to Apax ownership change in 2022, the Tanteo Spirits merger in 2023, the University of Tennessee Athletics partnership in 2024, and the Yee-Haw Brewing partnership to build a South Carolina distillery. Sources: https://www.apax.com/news-views/apax-funds-to-acquire-controlling-stake-in-ole-smoky-distillery-from-centerview-capita/ (11 February 2022), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230801838362/en/Ole-Smoky-Distillery-LLC-Announces-Merger-with-Tanteo-Spirits (1 August 2023), https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240821824723/en/Ole-Smoky-Distillery-Announces-Long-Term-Partnership-With-University-of-Tennessee-Athletics-as-The-Exclusive-Moonshine-of-the-Vols (21 August 2024), https://www.thespiritsbusiness.com/2025/02/ole-smoky-to-open-south-carolina-distillery-in-2026/ (February 2025).
- Ole Smoky joined the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States during his tenure. Source: https://olesmoky.com/blogs/news/ole-smoky-distillery-joins-distilled-spirits-council-of-the-united-states.

## What they say they care about

"Ole Smoky remains one of the fastest growing spirits companies in the country, and this would not have been possible without the passion and leadership of Michael Bender."
Source: https://craftspiritsmag.com/2024/06/04/ole-smoky-announces-transition-of-president-of-wholesale-into-strategic-advisory-role/ (4 June 2024).

His public record points at two things. **Growth framed through the wholesale organisation**, and **industry level responsibility work**, evidenced by chairing Responsibility.org and taking the company into DISCUS. He is the person in this history who institutionalised the compliance posture.

## How they would read a loyalty and CRM work sample

He is on his way out, so he is not the decision maker. He matters for a different reason: he is the reason the company's compliance instincts are conservative, and anyone interviewing will meet those instincts in the room whether or not he is in it. The Chair of Responsibility.org does not run a programme that rewards drinking frequency, time spent at a bar, or presence during a weather advisory. Anything in a loyalty design that pays for consumption behaviour will be read through that lens and will fail, and it will fail on culture rather than on law.

That has a direct design consequence worth stating explicitly in a work sample. A daily resetting quest slot creates an implied daily drinking cadence, and so nothing that resets daily may be tied to consumption, purchase, or presence at a drinking venue. The same logic replaces a reward for staying ninety minutes at a bar with a reward for logging a designated driver or a rideshare at check in. Showing that trade being made deliberately, and naming the reason, converts a compliance constraint into evidence of judgement.

The second thing he leaves behind is the wholesale orientation. He described growth through the wholesale organisation and its leaders. That is the incumbent mental model at the company, and a consumer first CRM programme is a change to it. Naming that as a change, rather than pretending it is a natural extension, is the honest framing.

## What makes them say no

- Any mechanic that rewards drinking more, drinking faster, or staying longer at a licensed venue.
- Anything that would embarrass the company inside DISCUS or Responsibility.org.
- Marketing to anyone whose age has not been verified. Tennessee makes 21 plus gating mandatory by rule under TABC 0100-06-.03(14)(f), not merely by industry code.
- Treating the wholesale organisation as an obstacle rather than a partner.
