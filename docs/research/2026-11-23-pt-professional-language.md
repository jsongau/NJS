# Professional personal training language (2026-11-23)

What established training brands and certifying bodies actually say, so NJS Fitness copy reads like a
professional coach and not a side hustle. Read before writing any `/fitness` copy. Quotes were read on
2026-11-23; re-check before relying on one that is more than a year old.

## 1. How the big clubs describe the service

| Source | Phrases they use |
|---|---|
| [Equinox, Personal Training](https://www.equinox.com/personal-training) | "Work 1:1 with a coach", "science-backed, personalized plan", "Every Coach. One Standard", "Your coach plans out every week of sessions and classes", "The Equifit assessment is the foundation of Personal Training" |
| [Life Time, Dynamic Personal Training](https://www.lifetime.life/locations/ny/midtown/training/dynamic-personal-training.html) | "an engaging, all-encompassing form of coaching", "trainer-guided", "a fully engaged training experience", "our monthly program rooted in science" |
| [Gordon College Balance, In-Home Training](https://balance.gordon.edu/in-home-training) | "Home Personal Training", "the nationally certified personal trainer and the necessary equipment to you", "in the comfort of the client's own home", a stated service radius |

Patterns worth copying:
- **Coach** and **1:1** carry more weight than "trainer" and "private". Equinox writes "coach" throughout.
- The first meeting is an **assessment** (Equinox: Equifit; Life Time: a trainer-guided cardio test). It is
  framed as "the foundation", not as a sales call.
- The plan is **personalized** and **planned week by week**. The program has a name and a length.
- In-home service is named as a service ("In-home personal training", "Home Personal Training"), with
  where it happens and how far the coach travels. Casual lines like "I come to you" or "we come to you"
  show up mostly on marketplace listings (Thumbtack, Bark), not on established brands.

## 2. Wording swaps for NJS Fitness

| Casual | Professional | Where it is used |
|---|---|---|
| I come to you | In-home training | Booking step place card (done 2026-11-23) |
| I come to you (heading) | In-home and on-site training | 12-pack "what it includes" card (done 2026-11-23) |
| when I come to you | for in-home training | FAQ, How do I pay (done 2026-11-23) |
| Your place | In-home | Candidate for the Where I train card |
| Train with me | Train 1:1 with me | Candidate, hero and packs |
| first session | first session and assessment | Candidate, only if an assessment really happens |
| A travel fee may apply | Travel within [radius] is included; beyond that, a travel fee applies | Booking note, once Jay sets a radius |

Do not borrow claims that need proof: "nationally certified", "science-backed", "the best in the business".
Use them only with the certificate or the study behind them (handbook rule 4, honest copy).

## 3. Professional conduct a client expects to see

From the [NASM Code of Professional Conduct](https://www.nasm.org/docs/default-source/PDF/nasm-code-of-professional-conduct.pdf?sfvrsn=2):

- **Safety first:** training starts only after a signed health-history questionnaire is reviewed; current
  CPR and AED certification; a session stops at once on unusual pain.
- **Scope of practice:** trainers do not "diagnose or treat illness or injury unless for basic first aid".
  Nutrition and supplement advice goes to a qualified professional unless the trainer holds that credential.
- **Referral:** refer to a medical practitioner on a change in health or medication, an undiagnosed injury
  or risk factor, or unusual pain.
- **Confidentiality:** client information stays private, in advertising too, unless the client agrees in writing.
  Records are kept and discarded securely.
- **Business:** honest advertising, qualifications represented accurately, adequate liability insurance.

The ACE Code of Ethics page (acefitness.org) did not load on 2026-11-23. Read it before quoting ACE.

What this means on the site:
- A "Before your first session" line (health questionnaire, CPR/AED) reads as professional and costs nothing.
  Add it only if Jay actually does both.
- Testimonials and progress photos need the client's written OK (confidentiality).
- Keep the fat-loss page line "a coach, not a dietitian": it matches scope of practice.
- No page says a session or treatment heals an injury (already the rule for coach's notes).
