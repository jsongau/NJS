Bands are light, cheap and go anywhere. Used with intent, they build real strength, which makes them the tool I reach for with at-home and online clients.

A full session

Six moves,head to toe.

Two or three rounds of 10 to 15 reps each. Pick a band that makes the last three reps hard with clean form.

01

Squat

Stand on the band, handles at your shoulders. Sit back and down, then stand tall.

02

Row

Anchor the band at chest height. Pull your elbows back and squeeze your shoulder blades.

03

Press

Anchor behind you, or stand on the band and press overhead. Ribs down, no leaning back.

04

Pull-apart

Arms straight in front, pull the band apart to your chest. Small move, strong upper back.

05

Hip hinge

Stand on the band and hold it at arm's length. Push your hips back, then drive them forward.

06

Anti-twist press

Anchor to one side, press the band straight out from your chest and resist the pull.

What the research says

Do bandsreally work?

01

As strong as weights, in studies

A review of seven studies found no difference in strength gains between elastic resistance and machines or free weights.

SAGE Open Medicine, 2019: review of seven studies

02

It counts toward the guideline

The CDC recommends muscle-strengthening work for all major muscle groups at least 2 days a week. Bands count.

CDC: adult activity guidelines

03

Where weights still win

Bands get harder as they stretch and are hard to load precisely. For heavy strength work, I add weights when you have them.
