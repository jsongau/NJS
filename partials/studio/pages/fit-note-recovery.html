The recovery room.What's worth it.

Cryotherapy, compression boots, red light, water massage beds: big gyms now have a whole room of them. Here is what each one is and what the research says, so you spend your time on what helps.

The tools

Five tools,ranked by evidence.

Best supported first. General information, not medical advice.

Best supported

Sports massage and percussion

What it is. Hands, or a massage gun, working the muscle after training.

What the research says. A review of 29 studies found massage improved soreness by about 13% and flexibility by about 7%. It did not improve strength, sprint or endurance.

BMJ Open Sport and Exercise Medicine: sports massage review
Before training, maybe

Red light therapy

What it is. Panels or beds of red and near-infrared light on the skin.

What the research says. A 2025 review found low-certainty evidence that it reduces next-day soreness when used before exercise, not after. Devices differ, so results may not carry over.

Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies, 2025
Relaxing, soreness unchanged

Compression boots (Normatec)

What it is. Sleeves that inflate and squeeze your legs in a wave, for 15 to 30 minutes.

What the research says. The same 2025 review found moderate-certainty evidence that pneumatic compression does not reduce soreness at 24 hours. Many people still find it relaxing.

Journal of Bodywork and Movement Therapies, 2025
Evidence is thin

Whole-body cryotherapy

What it is. Two to three minutes in a chamber of extremely cold air.

What the research says. A Cochrane review found the evidence insufficient to say it prevents or treats muscle soreness: four small studies, 64 people, very low quality. Its safety has not been well studied.

Cochrane: whole-body cryotherapy
Little independent research

HydroMassage beds

What it is. You lie clothed on a dry bed while jets of warm water travel under the surface.

What the research says. I found no independent trials of the beds themselves. The closest evidence is for massage in general, above. Treat it as a pleasant way to wind down.

My take as a coach

How I'd usethe room.

None of these replaces training, sleep and food. They are extras, and the best one is the one you enjoy enough to keep doing.

  1. Train first

    The session is what changes your body. Recovery tools help you feel ready for the next one.

  2. Massage after hard days

    Hands, a massage gun or the water bed. It has the best evidence for soreness.

  3. Boots to wind down

    Good for ten quiet minutes with your legs up. Do not expect less soreness tomorrow.

  4. Cold with care

    Whole-body cold is extreme. Follow the staff's rules, and ask your doctor first if you have a heart or circulation condition.
