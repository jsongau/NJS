The warm-up

Raise, open,switch on, rehearse.

About ten minutes in total. Keep every part easy enough to talk through.

  1. Raise · 2 to 3 minutes

    Jump rope, light shadow boxing or a brisk walk. You want to feel warm, not winded.

  2. Open · 3 minutes

    Moving stretches for the joints you are about to use: hip circles, leg swings, arm circles, a deep lunge with a reach.

  3. Switch on · 2 minutes

    Light work for the muscles that steady you: glute bridges, band pull-aparts, a plank.

  4. Rehearse · 2 to 3 minutes

    The first move of the session at an easy weight or pace. Empty bar squats before squats, slow jabs before pad work.

What the research says

Why bother,in three findings.

From a 2026 review of 40 studies. Most of that research was done in youth soccer, so read it as a strong hint, not a guarantee.

01

Fewer injuries

Structured warm-up programs were linked to a lower risk of lower-body injuries.

Sports (Basel), 2026: review of 40 studies

02

Move first, hold later

Long static holds, over 60 seconds, before activity were associated with worse performance. Moving warm-ups are the better choice beforehand.

Sports (Basel), 2026: review of 40 studies

03

Ten minutes is enough

One trial in the review found no significant difference between 10 and 20 minute warm-up sessions.

Sports (Basel), 2026: review of 40 studies
