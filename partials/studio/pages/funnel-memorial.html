          09 · The first 30 days

          What happens, in order.

          Families bookmark this in the worst week of their year. Months later, it is often why they come back to plan their own.

          Sources: Social Security; AARP, VA benefits; California small estates. General guidance, not legal advice.

          1. 1
            Call for helpIf the death was unexpected, 911. Under hospice, the hospice nurse.
          2. 2
            Call the funeral providerThey bring your loved one into their care. Nothing needs choosing tonight.
          3. 3
            Look for a planA prepaid plan, written wishes, or a policy. It answers most questions.
          4. 4
            Tell close familyOne person makes the calls, so nobody hears it secondhand.
          1. 5
            Arrangement meetingChoices and the itemized price list, in writing.
          2. 6
            Death certificatesOrder several certified copies; banks and insurers each want one.
          3. 7
            Social SecurityThe funeral director usually reports the death. A spouse who lived with them may get a 255-dollar payment.
          4. 8
            Veteran?Find the DD-214. Ask about national cemetery burial, a headstone and a flag.
          1. 9
            Life insurance and accountsClaims, and payable-on-death accounts with the beneficiary.
          2. 10
            The estateIn California, estates under 208,850 dollars may skip probate with the small estate affidavit, after a waiting period.
          3. 11
            Bills and subscriptionsCancel or transfer, and keep a list of every call.
          4. 12
            Grief supportAftercare notes at 30, 90 and 365 days, from people, not a sequence.
