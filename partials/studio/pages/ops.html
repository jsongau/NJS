Worked example · concept, not a client

Lead flow for a funeral planning company.

This is how I would set up the first 90 days for a memorial planning company with a sales team. The brand is made up. The method is the one I ran in dental.

Planning ahead

Calm, no clock.

Someone is arranging their own service, or a parent's, months or years early. They want prices, options and time. Ads offer a plain guide. Follow-up is slow and useful, and it stops the moment they ask.

A death has occurred

A person, right now.

The family needs help today. The phone number comes first and a person answers. No drip emails, no offers, no review request for weeks. The system's job here is to get out of the way and alert an agent.

The pipeline

  1. New inquiryForm, call or message. Tagged planning ahead or at need.
  2. ContactedText and email inside a minute. Call task for an agent.
  3. Appointment setCalendar booked. Reminders the day before and the hour before.
  4. Met with familyAgent logs the outcome in two taps.
  5. Plan signedCounts as the conversion sent back to Meta.
  6. Cared forCheck-in later. Review request only when it is kind to ask.

Follow-up for someone planning ahead

  1. Minute 1
    A text and an email from a named agent. "I have your request. Here is the price guide you asked for. Reply here or call me directly."
  2. Minute 5
    A call task lands with the agent. If nobody picks it up in fifteen minutes, the sales manager is told.
  3. Day 1
    The first decisions, in order. Burial or cremation, service or no service, who should be told. One short email.
  4. Day 3
    A check-in by text. One question, easy to answer with one word.
  5. Day 7
    Costs and payment plans. What families ask most, answered plainly.
  6. Day 14
    A last note, then quiet. After that, one useful email a month until they book or opt out.

Ads that respect the reader

Meta restricts ads that assert or imply something personal about the viewer, and grief is about as personal as it gets. So I write to the question, never to the person.

Not this "Did you just lose a parent?"

This "A simple guide to planning a service, with prices."

Rule: Meta Advertising Standards, personal attributes. Before launch I also check whether a campaign needs a special ad category, as insurance and financial products can.

Reviews, asked with care

A review request a week after a funeral reads as a company that forgot what just happened. Planning-ahead clients get asked after signing. At-need families get a hand-written check-in first, and a request only if the agent marks it right.

This is the same idea as the suppression rules I built in dental, where the treatment type decided who was asked.

The first 90 days

Days 1 to 30

Learn and audit

Sit with the sales team. Map every lead source to a pipeline stage. Check the pixel, the events and what Meta is being told a conversion is. Fix what is broken before touching budget.

Days 31 to 60

Tighten and test

Speed up first contact. Split planning ahead from at need. Start a creative test every week and the Monday report every Monday.

Days 61 to 90

Own and extend

Tune ads to signed plans, not form fills. Automate review requests with the right exclusions. Bring ownership two new ideas with numbers attached.
