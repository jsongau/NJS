# Changelog

One line per working day. Longer notes live in `docs/` as dated session files.

## 2026-08-14

A hundred and nine more organisations, found by industry rather than by map.
The first hundred and two came out of a sweep of the trade area, which is why
they were eighty per cent Brea and heavy on shopfronts and clinics; that is a
route, and the posting asks for a strategy. So nine sectors were named first,
chosen for being thin on the board with a real occasion behind them, and each
was then searched for organisations that actually exist in it. Every row was
geocoded through the US Census Bureau one address at a time. Fourteen were
then audited adversarially by loading the page behind every cited email and
searching it for the exact address string: all nine email-bearing rows in the
sample rendered theirs in plain text, two rows failed on something else and
were removed, and four decision-maker titles that described a different person
from the mailbox owner were corrected. Sixteen more organisations were
researched, found real and held off the board, which makes twenty-five
published removals against two hundred and eleven published rows. Every row
now carries a two-digit NAICS sector, a third cut across the same board, and
`/segments` ranks those sectors by a weighted sum of volume, occasion
certainty and reach with the weights exposed as a control, because a ranking
whose weighting cannot be argued with is an opinion with a number next to it.

## 2026-08-13 (second pass)

The arcade turn. The warm paper theme was a better paper and still a paper, so
the ground is now near black at hue 300 and every colour on it emits rather
than reflects. The theme is called The Cabinet and every value in it is
generated by `scripts/theme_cabinet.py` from an LCh request, not typed. The
surface ramp inverted, so `--surface-3` is now the lightest paper and still
sets the floor. The nine lane ramp went from a dichromatic separation of 8.85
to 10.00 while gaining full chroma, which is the opposite of what saturation
normally does and is the reason this is defensible rather than merely louder.
Twenty sections each got an identity triple, wired through one inherited custom
property so a new screen is one field rather than twenty cases. The compose
window became a split screen with the letter lit and visible on open, an
honestly disabled Resend control that prints the request it would post, and a
mailto that withdraws itself when the draft is too long to survive one. Two
bowling leagues form on a sixteen team ladder with no invented scores, because
the building has not opened.

## 2026-08-13

Retheme, a mega nav with a mobile drawer, and the supply side. The cool paper
palette became lane maple, pin white and brass; `--line-strong` went from
1.99:1 to 3.06:1 and the nine lane ramp from a dichromatic separation of 1.32
to 8.85, so colour finally does something for a colourblind reader instead of
only the glyph doing it. Six most-clicked destinations now sit above the rail
with their live counts, "Trade area" is "Maps" everywhere while the route
stays `/map`, and below 900px the rail folds into a focus trapped drawer. Three
new screens answer the Round1 Cerritos posting: `/partners` for supplier and
licensor relationships, `/promo` for sell through and the statement a licensor
actually receives, and `/spend` for budgets, purchase orders, invoices and
contract terms.

## 2026-08-11 (third pass)

The six things the owner asked for after reading the finished fourteen. The
board grew to 102 organisations, 33 of them local retail, food and auto
service researched by hand and geocoded through the US Census Bureau, with
nine more found, verified and deliberately kept off the board for want of a
confirmed street. A ninth lane, `local-retail-food`. An expanded three pane
map board at `/map` whose occasion segment drives the desk's own lane filter
rather than a second one beside it. A compose modal with four intents and an
attachable group quote link. The PinMark redrawn to hold up at 38px. And an
independent accessibility audit in a browser, which found that `line-height: 0`
had been collapsing every lane chip label on the landing page to nothing,
leaving nine filters separated by colour alone in an application built the way
it is because the owner is colourblind.
