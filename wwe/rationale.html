Product strategy · candidate concept

A waitlist is not an asset until it converts

Ringside x WWE, a working concept by Nathan J. Song for the Manager, Membership Growth role. The sweepstakes is the vehicle. The product is a conversion system that walks an owned fan audience from waitlist sign-up to paid membership, the exact motion the role owns. This is the thinking behind it, written the way I would present it in a weekly readout.

1The product challenge

How do you convert a large and growing waitlist into paid members using only owned channels, without spending the trust that got fans onto the list in the first place?

WWE opened the door on a paid fan membership this year, and the waitlist-to-founding-member motion is happening right now, which makes this the rare portfolio concept aimed at a live problem rather than a hypothetical one. The role is explicitly owned-channel: no paid media parachute, just email, SMS, web, app, and the landing pages between them. This page is one of those landing pages, built end to end.

2The hypothesis

Fans do not join memberships because they were persuaded. They join because a specific moment made not being a member feel expensive: the presale they missed, the drop that sold out, the seat they could not get.

The hypothesis: a waitlist converts when waiting is made visible and claiming is made cheap. Show the fan their place in line, let referrals move them up, attach a founding price that locks on claim, and put a sweepstakes on top so the funnel is worth visiting even for the fan who is not ready to pay. Every mechanic on this page exists to move one number: paid conversion from the waitlist.

3The system

A standard waitlist sends one launch email and prays. This one is built as a ladder, and every rung is an owned-channel touch:

The standard waitlist

  1. Sign upa form and a thank-you
  2. Silenceweeks of nothing
  3. One launch emailand a prayer to the open rate
Converts the fans who were already sold, loses everyone the silence cooled off.

This system

  1. Sweepstakes entry doubles as waitlist sign-upa reason to act today, not at launch
  2. A visible place in lineposition shown the moment you join
  3. Referrals move you upsharing pays in position and entries, costs the business nothing
  4. Weekly drawings keep the list warma real cutoff every Sunday, honest urgency on a schedule
  5. The founding price lockclaiming early is cheaper than waiting, visibly and permanently
  6. A 40-second claim flowplan, start date, lock, three choices, no dead ends
  7. Winners become the retention cohorta member with a year of receipts and zero sunk cost
Every week the list gets warmer or shorter, and both outcomes are wins.

Who gets what, and why each party stays in the loop:

The fanA free shot at the year, a visible place in line, and a price that rewards deciding early.The incentive and the fandom point the same direction, which is why this funnel can be sent to an owned list with a straight face.
The waitlistStops being a spreadsheet and becomes a game with weekly heartbeats.Position, referrals, and drawings give the lifecycle team a reason to email that is not "still coming soon."
WWEConverts owned attention into recurring membership revenue without a dollar of paid media.And every claim event, drop-off, and referral feeds the weekly readout that decides next week's test.

Even the prize obeys the loop: the winner's free year starts at claim, stamps a permanent member number, and gifts a second year to a friend. A prize that costs money is a liability; a prize that manufactures the cleanest retention cohort in the program is a research budget.

4The measurement plan

The job description lists the weekly readout, so this concept instruments exactly that list. Not page views, these:

Waitlist growthNet new sign-ups per week, split by source: organic, referral, sweepstakes entry. The referral share is the health metric.
Email engagementOpens and clicks per lifecycle send, benchmarked send over send. The weekly drawing gives every email a reason to exist.
Click-through and landing conversionList to landing, landing to entry. The A/B toggle on the hero is the first test in the queue, live-moment framing against stop-paying-per-event.
Checkout completionClaim-flow starts against claims finished. Three steps, each instrumented, so the drop-off names the fix.
Paid member conversionThe headline number: share of the waitlist converted to paid, weekly and cumulative, split by cohort and by segment answer from the quiz.
Retention and renewalFounding cohort against later cohorts, winners against payers. The winner cohort is the control group the program gets for free.

No projections appear anywhere in this concept on purpose. A growth candidate who invents his own conversion rates is auditioning for the wrong skill; the honest version is naming the instruments and the decision each one drives.

5The honest-growth line

A family entertainment brand cannot borrow dark patterns, so the whole system observes bright lines: the countdown is a real weekly cutoff, identical for every visitor. The sweepstakes is free, and buying changes no odds, with the mail-in path printed where people can see it. Scarcity is only invoked where it is true, the founding window really closes, which is why the page is allowed to say so. Growth that a TV-PG brand cannot put its name on is not growth, it is a liability on a delay.

6Design decisions worth defending

Entry and waitlist are one action.

Fans should never choose between the fun path and the useful path. One email field feeds both: the drawing entry and the held place in line. Every future lifecycle touch inherits that dual frame, you might win, and you are getting closer either way.

Plan size comes first in the claim flow.

Household size is the only question that changes the price, so it leads. The start-date step absorbs the biggest claim objection, wrong timing, by letting the year begin at the Rumble or Mania instead of today. Objection-ordered flows outperform information-ordered ones.

The winner state is designed, not implied.

Sweepstakes funnels design the entry and ignore the win. The toggle on the thank-you page shows both states because the winner cohort is the program's free retention experiment, and an unclaimed prize is a churned member who never even paid.

No streaming, and the page says so.

The shows live with broadcast partners, and a membership concept that pretended otherwise would prove the builder never checked. The benefit explorer covers what has no broadcast home: presales, drops, points, community, event day. Doing the homework is a feature.

Segments are collected as play.

Fan type, benefit priority, and household shape arrive through a three-tap quiz and an era grid instead of a survey. Those are the exact fields launch sequencing needs, gathered at the moment of highest interest and zero friction.

Accessibility is in the bones.

Real tablist keyboard patterns on the comparison and calendar modules, radiogroup semantics in the quiz, focus management in the claim flow, reduced-motion fallbacks everywhere, and the countdown is a labeled timer, not a flashing div.

7What I would test first

  • Hero framing: the live moment against the stop-paying-per-event pitch, the A/B toggle on the page is the visible version of that plan.
  • Claim timing: founding lock offered immediately after entry, or after the first referral, when investment is proven.
  • Position mechanics: does showing the exact waitlist number beat showing a percentile, and where does moving up stop motivating and start feeling rigged.
  • Winner framing: does "your free year starts when you claim" read as delightful or as strings attached. The copy that wins decides how the rules get written.

If the claim rate holds, this stops being one funnel and becomes the repeatable launch playbook: every future benefit drop, event push, and seasonal moment re-runs the same ladder with new stakes. The membership is the product; the ladder is the machine that sells it.
