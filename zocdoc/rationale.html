Design rationale · candidate concept

A toothpaste brand that pays people to see the dentist

NJS x Zocdoc, a product design concept by Nathan J. Song, built as a working page rather than a slide deck. This is the thinking behind it, written the way I would present it to a design crit.

1The brief I gave myself

Zocdoc's mission is to give power to the patient. The hard version of that problem is not search or booking UX, it is that millions of people simply never initiate care. Nobody wakes up wanting a dental checkup. People do wake up wanting whiter teeth.

So the brief: design a consumer product experience where wanting whiter teeth ends with a booked dentist appointment. A nano-hydroxyapatite toothpaste brand is the vehicle because its honest story, repair over bleach, points at dentistry instead of away from it.

2The loop

The sweepstakes rents attention. The booking handoff is the business:

NJSFunds a 20% subscription discount that activates only on a verified Zocdoc booking.It buys a customer who sees a dentist twice a year, the customer most likely to keep teeth, habits, and subscriptions.
The patientGets cheaper toothpaste for doing the thing their dentist already wanted.The incentive and the health advice point the same direction, which is why this loop can be marketed with a straight face.
ZocdocGets booking volume it did not pay to acquire, funded by a CPG marketing budget.A new supply-side pitch too: dentists gain a channel that delivers recurring, prevention-minded patients.

Even the prize obeys the loop: the winner’s year of NJS ships a season at a time, and shipment one unlocks at the first checkup booked through Zocdoc. A prize that costs money is a liability; a prize that walks its winner into a dentist’s chair is an asset with a marketing budget.

3Design decisions worth defending

Insurance-first booking.

The demo booking flow asks for insurance before showing dentists, mirroring the real product truth: on Zocdoc, coverage is the search filter that decides everything else. Putting the least glamorous question first is a sequencing decision, it prevents the heartbreak pattern of falling for a dentist you cannot afford.

The winner state is designed, not implied.

Sweepstakes funnels design the entry and ignore the win. The toggle on the thank-you page shows both states because the winner experience is where the retention mechanics live. Designing the 0.1% case is what separates a funnel that converts from a system that compounds.

Trust is sequenced, not sprinkled.

A free year of product triggers "what's the catch" in the first viewport, so the proof strip answers it in the same viewport. Then the page earns each claim in order: mechanism, ingredients, honest comparisons with real concessions, expectation timeline, objections. Every module concedes something true, fluoride's record, bleach's power, because a page that never admits anything is a page nobody believes.

Honest urgency only.

The countdown is a real weekly cutoff, Sunday 8 PM Pacific, identical for every visitor, never resetting per session. Deceptive timers convert until they get screenshotted. In healthcare-adjacent products the trust bar is higher, so the urgency pattern has to survive scrutiny, not just clicks.

Two brands, one token system.

The NJS mint theme and the Zocdoc co-brand theme are the same components under different design tokens. The toggle in the header is a working demonstration of how a co-branded partnership surface would ship without forking the codebase.

Accessibility is in the bones.

Real tablist keyboard patterns on the comparison and timeline modules, radiogroup semantics in the quiz, focus management into opened detail panels, reduced-motion fallbacks on every animation, and honest urgency doubling as an accessible one, the countdown is a labeled timer, not a flashing div.

4What I would test first

  • Repair framing against whitening framing in the hero, the A/B toggle on the page is the visible version of that plan.
  • Booking placement: post-entry only, or also as a pre-entry path for the audience that wants the discount more than the prize.
  • Discount depth: 10, 20, and 30 percent against six-month subscription retention, not against click-through.
  • Winner-loop framing: does "your first shipment unlocks at your first checkup" read as delightful or as strings attached? The copy that wins decides how the rules get written.

5What success looks like

Not entries. Booking attach rate: the share of entrants who complete a Zocdoc booking within 14 days. Cost per verified booking against Zocdoc's existing paid channels. Six-month subscription retention split by checkup completion, the number that proves the loop's thesis that patients who show up, stay.

If the attach rate holds above the cost of the discount, this stops being a toothpaste funnel and becomes a repeatable pattern: any health-adjacent consumer brand can subsidize its customers into care, through Zocdoc. Toothpaste and dentists is just the first pairing.
