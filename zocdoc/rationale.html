Product strategy · candidate concept

A patient acquisition engine, disguised as a toothpaste brand

NJS x Zocdoc, a working concept by Nathan J. Song. The toothpaste is the vehicle. The product is a system that routes a cosmetic desire, whiter teeth, into completed preventive care, and turns a CPG marketing budget into a patient acquisition channel for Zocdoc. This is the thinking behind it, written the way I would present it to a product review.

1The product challenge

How might a consumer wellness product increase preventive dental visits while creating a patient acquisition channel that providers and Zocdoc do not have to pay for?

Zocdoc's mission is to give power to the patient. The hard version of that problem is not search UX or booking UX, it is that millions of people never initiate care at all. Acquisition today is bought with ads and search. This concept explores a third channel: consumer products whose own economics improve when their customers see a doctor.

2The hypothesis

Nobody wakes up wanting a dental checkup. People wake up wanting whiter teeth. Those two desires are usually served by different industries that never speak, cosmetics on one side, dentistry on the other.

The hypothesis: if the commercial incentive and the clinical advice point in the same direction, vanity can be made to fund prevention. A nano-hydroxyapatite toothpaste brand is the right vehicle because its honest story, repair over bleach, points at dentistry instead of away from it. The test is whether a cosmetic desire can be routed into a completed appointment without a single deceptive step along the way.

3The system

A standard CPG funnel ends at the receipt. This one is built as a loop, and the last step funds the first:

The standard CPG funnel

  1. Paid adsattention, bought at market rate
  2. Landing pageone persuasive surface
  3. One purchaseand a follow-up email
Ends at the receipt. The next customer is bought all over again, at full price.

This system

  1. Creator storefronts and a sweepstakesattention arrives through people, not just ads
  2. Education that earns each claimmechanism, evidence, honest concessions
  3. Insurance-first booking on Zocdoccoverage asked before dentists are shown
  4. A completed checkupverified by the appointment, not a promise
  5. 20% subscription unlockthe discount activates on the booking
  6. Referral and creator commissionpaid on product, never on appointments
  7. Repeat care, twice a yearthe checkup cadence becomes the retention cadence
Loops. Every kept subscription funds the next booking incentive.

Who funds what, and why each party stays in the loop:

NJSFunds a 20% subscription discount that activates only on a verified Zocdoc booking.It buys a customer who sees a dentist twice a year, the customer most likely to keep teeth, habits, and subscriptions.
The patientGets cheaper toothpaste for doing the thing their dentist already wanted.The incentive and the health advice point the same direction, which is why this loop can be marketed with a straight face.
ZocdocGets booking volume it did not pay to acquire, funded by a CPG marketing budget.A new supply-side pitch too: dentists gain a channel that delivers recurring, prevention-minded patients.

Even the prize obeys the loop: the winner’s year of NJS ships a season at a time, and shipment one unlocks at the first checkup booked through Zocdoc. A prize that costs money is a liability; a prize that walks its winner into a dentist’s chair is an asset with a marketing budget.

4Five stakeholders, one loop

This was designed as an ecosystem, not a page. Five parties, each with a reason to stay:

The creator layer is the acquisition engine's front door: three working storefronts, the Smile Circle, where audiences arrive already trusting the person who invited them, and every checkout closes with a Zocdoc booking CTA.

5The measurement plan

Not page views, and not entries. These are the instruments I would wire before launch:

Booking attach rateThe share of entrants who complete a Zocdoc booking within 14 days. The headline number for the whole concept.
Cost per verified bookingMeasured against Zocdoc's existing paid channels. If this channel is not cheaper, the loop does not deserve to exist.
Subscription activationThe share of bookers who turn the 20% unlock into an active subscription, the moment NJS gets paid back.
Six-month retention, split by checkupSubscribers who completed a checkup versus those who did not. The number that proves or kills the thesis that patients who show up, stay.
Referral participationThe share of subscribers who bring in at least one entrant, the loop's organic growth coefficient.
Creator conversionBookings and product sales per storefront session, per creator. Ranks the Smile Circle and decides who gets the next storefront.

No projections appear anywhere in this concept on purpose. A portfolio piece that invents its own results would fail the exact trust bar the page argues for; the honest version is naming the instruments and the decision each one drives.

6The compliance line

A healthcare marketplace cannot borrow growth mechanics blindly, so the whole system observes one bright line: creators earn commission on product, never on appointments. The booking discount rewards the patient directly, no influencer is paid per checkup, and the funnel's urgency is real, one weekly cutoff, identical for every visitor. Growth that a healthcare brand cannot put its name on is not growth, it is a liability on a delay.

7Design decisions worth defending

Insurance-first booking.

The demo booking flow asks for insurance before showing dentists, mirroring the real product truth: on Zocdoc, coverage is the search filter that decides everything else. Putting the least glamorous question first is a sequencing decision, it prevents the heartbreak pattern of falling for a dentist you cannot afford.

The winner state is designed, not implied.

Sweepstakes funnels design the entry and ignore the win. The toggle on the thank-you page shows both states because the winner experience is where the retention mechanics live. Designing the 0.1% case is what separates a funnel that converts from a system that compounds.

Trust is sequenced, not sprinkled.

A free year of product triggers "what's the catch" in the first viewport, so the proof strip answers it in the same viewport. Then the page earns each claim in order: mechanism, ingredients, honest comparisons with real concessions, expectation timeline, objections. Every module concedes something true, fluoride's record, bleach's power, because a page that never admits anything is a page nobody believes.

Honest urgency only.

The countdown is a real weekly cutoff, Sunday 8 PM Pacific, identical for every visitor, never resetting per session. Deceptive timers convert until they get screenshotted. In healthcare-adjacent products the trust bar is higher, so the urgency pattern has to survive scrutiny, not just clicks.

Two brands, one token system.

The NJS theme and the Zocdoc co-brand theme are the same components under different design tokens, and the co-brand surface ships behind a URL parameter. A working demonstration of how a partnership page would launch without forking the codebase.

Accessibility is in the bones.

Real tablist keyboard patterns on the comparison and timeline modules, radiogroup semantics in the quiz, focus management into opened detail panels, reduced-motion fallbacks on every animation, and honest urgency doubling as an accessible one, the countdown is a labeled timer, not a flashing div.

8What I would test first

  • Repair framing against whitening framing in the hero, the A/B toggle on the page is the visible version of that plan.
  • Booking placement: post-entry only, or also as a pre-entry path for the audience that wants the discount more than the prize.
  • Discount depth: 10, 20, and 30 percent against six-month subscription retention, not against click-through.
  • Winner-loop framing: does "your first shipment unlocks at your first checkup" read as delightful or as strings attached? The copy that wins decides how the rules get written.

If the attach rate holds above the cost of the discount, this stops being a toothpaste funnel and becomes a repeatable pattern: any health-adjacent consumer brand can subsidize its customers into care, through Zocdoc. Toothpaste and dentists is just the first pairing.
